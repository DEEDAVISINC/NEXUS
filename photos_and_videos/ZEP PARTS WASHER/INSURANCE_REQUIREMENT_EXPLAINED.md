## 💡 THE ANSWER: YOU'RE NOT JUST "PLACING AN ORDER"

**What the municipality sees you as:**
- ✅ A **vendor/contractor** providing goods
- ✅ A **supplier** delivering equipment to their facility
- ✅ A **business** entering their property
- ✅ Someone who could cause **liability exposure**

**What you think you're doing:**
- "I'm just ordering from Zoro and having it delivered"

**What you're ACTUALLY doing (in legal terms):**
- You're a **prime contractor** to the City of Warren
- You're responsible for the product from purchase → delivery → warranty
- You're entering a municipal facility (DPW garage)
- You're providing industrial equipment that could malfunction

---

## ⚠️ WHY MUNICIPALITIES REQUIRE THIS:

### **Scenario 1: Product Liability**
- You deliver ZEP parts washer
- 6 months later, it malfunctions and catches fire
- Burns down part of the DPW garage
- Warren sues YOU (the contractor), not Zoro
- Your insurance covers Warren's loss

### **Scenario 2: Delivery Injury**
- Your delivery person drops the washer
- Injures a Warren city employee
- Employee sues
- Your insurance covers it

### **Scenario 3: Property Damage**
- During delivery, your truck damages Warren's building
- Or the parts washer leaks and damages their floor
- Warren claims against YOUR insurance

### **Scenario 4: Product Defect**
- Filter bags are defective
- Cause chemical spill in Warren facility
- Environmental cleanup costs $50K
- Warren comes after YOU first (you're their contractor)

**Every government contract (federal, state, local) requires insurance:**

**Typical Requirements:**
- Small contracts (<$25K): $1M general liability

- Large contracts (>$100K): $2M-$5M general liability
