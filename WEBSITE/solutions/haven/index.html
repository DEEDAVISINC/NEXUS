The Problem

The Crisis No One Is Ready For

When disaster strikes, enrolled plan members are displaced — housing, transportation, and medical services fail simultaneously. MCOs remain accountable for continuity even when local networks collapse.

🏠

Housing Collapses

Members scatter to shelters, hotels, relatives — no stable address for care delivery. Home destroyed or uninhabitable. Evacuated 100+ miles away. Care coordination becomes impossible.

🚗

Transport Disappears

Members can’t reach appointments — their car is gone, routine NEMT providers are overwhelmed or shut down. Unfamiliar area, no options. Dialysis transport breaks down entirely.

💊

Medical Continuity Breaks

Critical services interrupted — meds, dialysis, home health, DME all disrupted. Prescriptions lost or expired. Dialysis center flooded. Home health aide can’t reach the member.

The Regulatory Reality: MCOs remain responsible for member continuity regardless of disaster. CMS Section 1135 waivers provide flexibility, but the obligation to provide care doesn’t disappear. Every missed dialysis treatment, every empty prescription, every unreachable member is a compliance risk.

The Solution

One TPA. Three Pillars. Complete Continuity.

DDI serves as the single coordination point — one contract with pre-built networks across housing, transportation, and medical services, ready to activate when disaster displaces plan members.

Service Pillars

Three Pillars. One TPA Relationship.

🏠

Housing Services

Without a stable address, care coordination collapses. Housing is the foundation that makes the other two pillars possible.

  • Hotel placement (individual and family rooms)
  • Extended stay facilities for longer displacement
  • Short-term rental coordination
  • ADA-accessible housing for mobility-impaired
  • Pet-friendly options
  • Duration tracking and extension management
  • FEMA TSA coordination where applicable
500–2,000Member placements per major event • 14–45 avg days • $150–$250/night negotiated
🚗

Transportation Services

Disaster-mode continuity transport for displaced members — not routine plan NEMT. The bridge between where members are evacuated and where care is available.

  • Pre-disaster evacuation (vulnerable populations)
  • Medical appointment transport
  • Dialysis transport (3x weekly, critical)
  • Pharmacy pickup/delivery coordination
  • Wheelchair and stretcher transport
  • Return transport when areas reopen
  • Charter bus for mass evacuation
2,000–10,000Transport trips per major event • 30–90 days elevated demand • $25–$75/trip avg
💊

Medical Continuity Services

Dialysis patients can’t miss treatments. Oxygen-dependent members can’t survive without their concentrator. Medical continuity is the reason HAVEN exists.

  • Prescription refills and emergency fills
  • Prior authorization overrides (Section 1135)
  • Pharmacy delivery to temporary locations
  • DME replacement (oxygen, wheelchairs, CPAP)
  • Home health aide reassignment
  • Dialysis center coordination (national networks)
  • Specialty care provider coordination
500–2,000Rx cases • 100–500 DME replacements • 200–800 home health reassignments per event
Real-World Scenario

All Three Pillars in Action

Maria, 67 — Dialysis Patient, Oxygen-Dependent

Maria lives in Houston. She receives dialysis 3x weekly and uses supplemental oxygen. A hurricane makes landfall. Her apartment floods. She evacuates to her daughter’s house in Dallas, 240 miles away.

🏠

Housing

Maria is at daughter’s house — stable address confirmed. HAVEN logs location, updates member record, enables care delivery.

🚗

Transport

Maria’s car is flooded. HAVEN schedules medical transport for dialysis 3x weekly at Dallas center. Rides confirmed for next 30 days.

💊

Medical

HAVEN coordinates dialysis at an alternate Dallas center. Portable oxygen concentrator delivered. Prescriptions transferred to Dallas pharmacy same day.

OutcomeWithout HAVENWith HAVEN
Dialysis continuityMisses 2 treatments → ER visit ($5,000+)Uninterrupted dialysis at alternate center
Oxygen supplyWithout concentrator → hospitalization ($15,000+)Portable O2 delivered within 24 hours ($200)

The Math: One member, one disaster, $19,000+ in avoided costs. Multiply by 500 high-risk members per MCO per event. HAVEN doesn’t cost money — it saves money.
