## 1. Before You Start — Compliance

### HIPAA / PII / PHI

Every time you open SHIELD, you will see a compliance acknowledgment screen. You must accept it to proceed. This is not a formality.

**You are handling:**
- Protected Health Information (PHI) — blood lead levels, medical records, test results
- Personally Identifiable Information (PII) — names, addresses, phone numbers, insurance details
- Children's information — subject to additional protections

**Rules:**
- Never share family data with anyone outside of SHIELD, your supervisor, or the assigned care team
- Never screenshot or photograph family records on your personal device
- Never discuss case details in public or on personal social media
- Never send PHI through personal email or text — use SHIELD's built-in SMS and logging tools only
- Lock your device when stepping away
- Report any data breach or suspected breach to your supervisor immediately

**Violation of HIPAA can result in termination and federal penalties up to $250,000 and imprisonment.**

## 2. Logging In

1. Open your browser and go to the SHIELD URL provided by your supervisor
2. Navigate to `/navigator`
3. Enter your **full name** and **CWC email address** (e.g., `angela.johnson@cwcare.org`)
4. Click **Sign In**
5. Accept the HIPAA compliance acknowledgment

If you see an error, check your email spelling. If the problem persists, contact your supervisor — your account may not be set up yet.

**Do not share your login with anyone. Do not log in on shared or public computers.**

## 4. Managing Your Caseload

### Viewing Families

Click **My Caseload** to see all families assigned to you. Each card shows:
- Family name and case number (e.g., SHD-2026-0001)
- County
- Urgency level (Standard, Urgent, Emergency)
- Current status and stage
- Services requested
- SLA countdown bar

### Urgency Levels

| Level | Meaning | Response Time |
|-------|---------|--------------|
| **Standard** | BLL < 5 µg/dL or routine screening | 72 hours |
| **Urgent** | BLL 5–14 µg/dL, elevated but stable | 24 hours |
| **Emergency** | BLL ≥ 15 µg/dL, housing displacement, or immediate safety risk | 4 hours |

The SLA bar at the bottom of each card shows how much time you have. Green = on track. Yellow = getting close. Red = overdue.

**If a case goes red, your supervisor is automatically notified.**

### Case Stages

Every referral moves through these stages:

```
Intake → Outreach → Engaged → In Service → Closed
```

- **Intake** — Referral received, not yet contacted
- **Outreach** — You're reaching out to the family
- **Engaged** — Family responded, needs assessment happening
- **In Service** — Services are active and being delivered
- **Closed** — All services complete, outcomes documented

## 5. Working a Case — Step by Step

When a new case appears in your caseload:

### Step 1: Review the Referral
Click the case card. Read:
- Who referred the family (caseworker name, agency)
- What services were requested
- How many children, their ages, and BLL levels if known
- County and address
- Any notes from the caseworker

### Step 2: First Contact
- Call the family within the SLA window (see urgency table above)
- Introduce yourself: *"Hi, this is [your name] from Cause We Care. I'm calling because [referring agency] submitted a referral for your family. I'm here to help coordinate services for your child."*
- Log the call in the Phone section (see Section 10)

### Step 3: Needs Assessment
During your first conversation or home visit:
- Confirm child information (name, age, test results)
- Verify address, insurance, and preferred language
- Ask about immediate needs: housing stability, transportation, food
- Identify which services to activate

### Step 4: Request Services
Go to the case detail view, scroll to **Active Services**, click **+ Add Service**, and select the appropriate services. These go to your supervisor for approval before activating (see Section 7).

### Step 5: Coordinate & Follow Up
- Schedule appointments (BLL tests, home visits, etc.)
- Arrange transportation (NEMT) if needed
- Check in with the family at least weekly while services are active
- Log every interaction — every call, text, note, and visit

### Step 6: Close the Case
When all services are complete and verified:
- Document outcomes (BLL at close, services delivered, housing status)
- Your supervisor will close the case after review

## 6. The 9 Service Lines

These are the services you coordinate for families. You don't deliver all of them yourself — some are performed by subcontractors or state programs. Your job is to **coordinate, track, and verify**.

### Services YOU Deliver Directly

| Service | What You Do |
|---------|------------|
| **Community Health Worker Home Visit** | Conduct in-home needs assessments, environmental scans, education on lead prevention, follow-up visits. Billable under Medicaid CHW codes. |
| **MIBridges Benefits Navigation** | Help families apply for SNAP, Medicaid, child care, cash assistance, and emergency relief through the MIBridges portal. |
| **Housing Navigation** | Emergency/temporary housing during remediation. Connect families to MSHDA programs, HUD, shelters. |

### Services You COORDINATE (Subcontractors / State Programs)

| Service | Who Does It | Your Role |
|---------|------------|-----------|
| **Blood Lead Level (BLL) Testing** | Quest Diagnostics or Local Health Dept lab | Schedule the test, arrange transport, track results, upload to SHIELD |
| **NEMT — Non-Emergency Medical Transportation** | DDI / Uber Health | Schedule rides to appointments, authorize in SHIELD, track completion |
| **CLPPP Case Management** | MDHHS Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention Program | Identify eligible children (BLL ≥ 5), submit referral, track enrollment, coordinate with CLPPP case manager |
| **Lead Remediation Coordination** | Certified abatement contractors | Schedule inspection, track remediation, confirm clearance, assist family during displacement |
| **Nurse Home Visit** | Licensed home health agency | Schedule visit, coordinate with family, track outcomes, follow up on clinical recommendations |
| **Filter Safety Net / Drinking Water** | MDHHS "Get Ahead of Lead" program | Enroll family, coordinate filter delivery/installation, verify usage, schedule replacements |

## 7. Requesting Services

You **cannot** activate services on your own. Here's the process:

1. Open the family's case
2. Scroll to **Active Services**
3. Click **+ Add Service**
4. Select the service from the list
5. The service appears as **PENDING APPROVAL**
6. Your supervisor reviews and approves or denies
7. Once approved, the status changes to **Active** and the verification workflow begins

**Why?** This prevents unauthorized service activation and ensures billing accuracy. Every active service generates a billing record — supervisors are the gate.

## 12. Activity Logging & Time Tracking

SHIELD tracks your time two ways:

### Automatic Tracking
The system automatically records time you spend working in SHIELD:
- Opening and reviewing a case (case review time)
- Making calls (logged with duration)
- Sending messages
- Completing verification steps

You don't need to do anything for this. It happens in the background.

### Field Work Timer (Manual)
For work outside the system — home visits, in-person appointments, community outreach:

1. Go to the **Time Log** section
2. Select the activity type (e.g., "CHW Home Visit", "NEMT Trip", "Benefits Enrollment")
3. Click **Start Timer** when you begin
4. Click **Stop Timer** when you finish
5. Add a note about what you did
6. The entry is saved with the duration, CPT code, and billing type

### How Time Becomes Billing
Every time entry is tagged with:
- **CPT code** — The medical billing code (e.g., 98960 for CHW services)
- **Billing type** — Who pays (e.g., Medicaid CHW, NEMT, Admin Fee)
- **Evidence** — What triggered the entry (case opened, call logged, timer started)

Your supervisor reviews time entries before they go to billing. Flagged entries may require explanation.

### Status Badges
Each time entry has a status:
- **LOGGED** — Recorded, waiting for supervisor review
- **VERIFIED** — Supervisor confirmed the entry
- **BILLED** — Sent to billing (VERTEX)
- **FLAGGED** — Supervisor has a question about this entry

## 14. Service Verification — How Billing Works

This is critical to understand. **No service is billed until it's verified.**

### The Verification Chain

```
Service Requested → Supervisor Approves → Service Begins → Verification → Billing
```

For each service activation, there are verification steps. For example, a Lead Remediation might require:

1. **Navigator confirms** — You verify the contractor was dispatched
2. **Contractor confirms** — The contractor replies via SMS: "DONE"
3. **Family confirms** — The family replies via SMS: "YES" to confirm service received

### Your Role in Verification

In the case detail view, each service shows a verification progress bar. You'll see buttons for:
- **Complete** — For steps you own (navigator verification)
- **Send SMS** — For steps that require contractor or family confirmation

When all steps are complete, the service moves to **Verified Complete** and a billing record is automatically created.

### What If Something Goes Wrong?
- If a contractor says "DONE" but the family says "NO" — the system flags it for investigation. Do not mark it as complete.
- If a family doesn't respond to the verification SMS — follow up by phone. Document the outcome.
- If you're unsure whether a service was actually delivered — **do not verify it**. Escalate to your supervisor.

**Fraudulent verification is grounds for immediate termination.**

## 15. Escalations & SLA

### SLA (Service Level Agreements)

Every referral has an SLA — a deadline for first contact based on urgency:
- **Standard**: 72 hours
- **Urgent**: 24 hours
- **Emergency**: 4 hours

The SLA bar on each case shows your remaining time.

### What Happens When SLA Is Breached?
1. Your supervisor receives an automatic alert
2. The case is flagged on the MDHHS partner dashboard
3. You'll need to document why the SLA was missed

### When to Escalate
Notify your supervisor immediately if:
- A child has a BLL ≥ 15 µg/dL and no services are active
- A family is being displaced and has nowhere to go
- A contractor fails to show up for a scheduled service
- A family reports a new emergency (hospitalization, eviction)
- You cannot reach a family after 3 contact attempts
- You suspect abuse or neglect (you are a mandatory reporter under Michigan law)

## 16. Do's and Don'ts

### Do
- Log every interaction — calls, texts, notes, visits
- Follow up with families at least weekly during active services
- Use SHIELD's built-in tools for all communication
- Set your status accurately
- Start your field work timer before every visit
- Read the case file before contacting a family
- Treat families with dignity and respect — they're going through a difficult time
- Ask for help when you're unsure

### Don't
- Don't verify a service you haven't confirmed actually happened
- Don't skip the call log — every call matters for billing and compliance
- Don't use personal email or text for family communication
- Don't share your login credentials
- Don't discuss cases in public
- Don't ignore the SLA bar — it's there for a reason
- Don't make promises you can't keep (e.g., "We'll get your house fixed by Friday")
- Don't diagnose or give medical advice — you're a navigator, not a clinician

## 17. Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **BLL** | Blood Lead Level — measured in micrograms per deciliter (µg/dL). ≥ 5 µg/dL is elevated. |
| **CLPPP** | Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention Program — state program for children with elevated BLL. |
| **CHW** | Community Health Worker — your role. Billable under Medicaid CPT 98960-98962. |
| **CPT Code** | Current Procedural Terminology — the billing code assigned to services. |
| **DDI** | Dee Davis Inc — the parent company operating SHIELD and CWC. |
| **CWC** | Cause We Care / Community Wellness Connections — the nonprofit arm delivering navigator services. |
| **EBL** | Elevated Blood Lead — BLL ≥ 5 µg/dL. |
| **MDHHS** | Michigan Department of Health and Human Services. |
| **MIBridges** | Michigan's online portal for public assistance applications. |
| **MSHDA** | Michigan State Housing Development Authority. |
| **NEMT** | Non-Emergency Medical Transportation — rides to/from appointments. |
| **PHI** | Protected Health Information — any health data tied to an individual. |
| **PII** | Personally Identifiable Information — name, address, SSN, etc. |
| **SLA** | Service Level Agreement — the deadline for first contact. |
| **SHIELD** | Screening, Housing, Intake, Education, Lead Defense — the system you're using. |
| **VERTEX** | DDI's billing system. You don't interact with it directly — it runs behind SHIELD. |
