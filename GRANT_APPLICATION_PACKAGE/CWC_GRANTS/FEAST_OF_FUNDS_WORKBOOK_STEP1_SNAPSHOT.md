## QUESTION 1 — What program or service do you want to grow or fund?

### COPYABLE

```
We want to grow two connected programs:

(1) SHIELD — Childhood Lead-Safe Navigation: schedules mandatory blood lead tests (ages 12 and 24 months), and when a child tests elevated, activates a full family support cascade (community health worker home visits, MIBridges benefits enrollment, medical transportation, CLPPP coordination, remediation and housing navigation, verified retest). Runs on Dee Davis Inc.’s SHIELD platform with PRISM field verification and VERTEX billing.

(2) HAVEN — Housing, Assistance, Vital Emergency Network: disaster-continuity TPA for displaced plan members and crisis-affected families. HAVEN is not daily Medicaid rides — it is pre-staged continuity when networks break: temporary housing coordination (hotels, short-term rentals, FEMA transitional shelter paths), evacuation and medical/pharmacy transport for displaced members, and medical continuity (emergency Rx, DME replacement, home health at a new location). Everything is pre-credentialed so we are not scrambling vendors after a disaster — we activate from member lists MCOs and partners send us.

Both programs sit on the same live infrastructure: DDI already operates Michigan MCO NEMT for HAP CareSource (Vendor ID 100000469269, Wayne + Macomb, ~4,500 dual-eligible members, portal live). HAVEN extends that proven TPA model into disaster mode; SHIELD extends it into childhood lead and family navigation in Southeast Michigan (Wayne first, then Oakland, Macomb, Genesee).
```

## QUESTION 2 — Who are the main people or communities you serve?

### COPYABLE

```
SHIELD:
• Young children (especially 12- and 24-month-olds) and families in Southeast Michigan affected by lead exposure, starting in Wayne County
• Low-income families needing Medicaid, SNAP, energy assistance, and related benefits (Cause We Care — MDHHS MI Bridges Community Partner since 2020)

HAVEN (separate population — do not mix with routine NEMT):
• Displaced plan members and crisis-affected households after hurricanes, floods, fires, or other disasters — people who lost normal housing, pharmacies, and local providers
• Medicaid and managed-care members who still need medical appointments, prescriptions, DME (oxygen, wheelchairs, CPAP), and home health after relocation
• Veterans and families in housing instability (Hair Cuts for Vets, coordinated entry) who need the same continuity principles outside a FEMA event

Plan NEMT (proof anchor for HAVEN):
• Dual-eligible and HIDE SNP Medicaid/MCO members with authorized non-emergency medical trips (dialysis, therapy, prenatal/postpartum, pharmacy-related medical transport) — Wayne and Macomb today

We serve these lanes separately on paper and in operations so funders see precise populations, not one vague “transport” bucket.
```

## QUESTION 3 — What changes do you want to see when this program is working well?

### COPYABLE

```
SHIELD:
• More children receive mandatory lead tests on time — moving toward universal testing, not ~20% tested today
• Families with elevated lead reach a navigator within 48 hours; 90%+ complete testing within 14 days; remediation, benefits, housing, and retest under 90 days on average
• One trusted door (Cause We Care navigators) instead of ten agencies; MDHHS sees live SLAs — 85%+ services verified complete, 95%+ clean billing, 4.0+/5.0 family satisfaction at closure

HAVEN:
• After a disaster declaration, displaced members still get shelter, rides to care, and medications — the network was live before the storm, not built in chaos afterward
• Health plans and MCOs send displaced-member lists; DDI coordinates housing placement, continuity transport, and Rx/DME/home health without members dying from interruption of care
• Pre-credentialed partners (housing, transport, pharmacy/DME/home health) activate from a registry — no post-disaster vendor scramble
• Documented continuity metrics (time to first shelter contact, trip completion for displaced members, emergency Rx/DME fulfillment) become proof for MCO disaster clauses and state/FEMA partnerships

Together:
• Medicaid members keep everyday appointments because plan NEMT runs reliably (live HAP operations)
• When crisis hits, the same TPA pushes HAVEN — continuity does not stop because networks broke
```

## QUESTION 4 — If you had a 6-figure package ($250K–$500K), what would you do in the next 12–18 months?

### COPYABLE

```
Months 0–6 (prove the system — SHIELD + HAVEN foundation):
• Proof largely delivered: HAP NEMT live + MDHHS SHIELD package (proposal, fulfillment map, 4 demo videos, May 2026); optional one-page funder PDF from same materials for MHEF/CFSEM only
• SHIELD: hire 3–5 Cause We Care CHW navigators + part-time supervisor; ramp Wayne County to ~25% mandatory testing capture; complete MDHHS vendor introductions for all 9 SHIELD service lines; VERTEX billing live on SHIELD and NEMT
• HAVEN Phase 1–2: build pre-credentialed partner registry (housing, charter/NEMT transport, mail-order pharmacy/DME/home health); draft MCO disaster-continuity contract language; run manual test dispatch from a displaced-member list (tabletop + small pilot with one MCO or state EM contact); document HAP trip/SLA data as the credibility deck — “same TPA, disaster mode”

Months 6–12 (grow what works):
• SHIELD public KPIs: <48 hr first contact, 90%+ tested in 14 days, 85%+ verified completion; add 2–3 navigators; start Oakland only after Wayne is stable
• HAVEN: add HAVEN rider language to every new MCO contract; credential 1–2 additional Michigan MCO NEMT lines using HAP proof; prepare hurricane-prone state MCO outreach (Florida/Louisiana entity work if pitching there)
• NEMT: second six-figure MCO relationship; never pitch HAVEN as “another courier” — pitch continuity of care for displaced populations

Months 12–18 (compound):
• SHIELD: expand toward Macomb or Genesee (Flint — highest need) if metrics hold; dedicated program manager for SHIELD/NEMT/HAVEN so growth is operational
• HAVEN: first paid continuity event or funded pilot (Phase 3 billing flows); monitoring toward push-button activation on declaration (roadmap Phase 4)
• Use 12–18 months of outcomes data for larger foundation and state funding and additional MCO disaster addenda

We would not spray nationwide in year one — we prove SHIELD and HAVEN on Michigan infrastructure, then scale.
```
