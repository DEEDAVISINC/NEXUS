## 1 — Clear Theory of Change

**Question:** Can you articulate the problem, your approach, and expected outcomes clearly?

### COPYABLE

```
PROBLEM:
• Michigan mandates blood lead testing at 12 and 24 months, but only ~20% of children are tested statewide — families fall through cracks.
• When a child tests elevated, families juggle abatement, housing, benefits, transportation, CLPPP, and retesting alone — or overstretched local health departments coordinate piecemeal.
• Medicaid members lose medical care when transportation fails or when disasters break housing, pharmacies, and home health — separate from “daily” plan NEMT gaps.

APPROACH (Cause We Care + program systems):
• SHIELD — one door for lead: schedule mandatory tests, and when BLL is elevated, activate a verified cascade (CHW home visit, MIBridges, NEMT, CLPPP, remediation, housing, retest) on the SHIELD platform with real-time MDHHS visibility.
• Live NEMT operations (HAP CareSource, Wayne + Macomb) prove we can run population programs at scale today.
• HAVEN — pre-credentialed disaster continuity (housing, medical transport, Rx/DME/home health) when MCOs send displaced-member lists — same TPA infrastructure, disaster mode.

EXPECTED OUTCOMES:
• 90%+ of referred families tested within 14 days; <48 hr first contact on elevated cases; <90 days to retest on average.
• 85%+ services verified complete; 95%+ clean claims; 4.0+ family satisfaction at closure.
• After disasters: displaced members retain shelter, care trips, and medications — documented continuity metrics for payers and funders.
```

### SHORT

```
Problem: lead testing gaps + fragmented elevated-BLL follow-up + broken care after transport/disaster failures. Approach: SHIELD one-door navigation, live NEMT proof, HAVEN continuity. Outcomes: on-time testing, closed-loop remediation, live accountability data.
```

**Readiness:** ✅ **Strong** — aligned with MDHHS Environmental Health meeting (Apr 23, 2026) and PA 146 of 2023 timing.
