# 🎯 Dealing with Non-Responsive Suppliers

**The Pattern:** Email/fax = ignored. Phone call + persistence = quote.

**Your Reality:** National Auto Fleet Group, and others, ignore written requests until you call and "make a scene."

---

## 🚨 The Problem

**What happens:**
1. You send professional email/fax with specs
2. Silence... days pass
3. You call and push hard
4. Suddenly they "find" your request and send a quote

**Why this happens:**
- They're busy/overwhelmed
- Government quotes = low priority (seen as slow, bureaucratic)
- Email/fax = easy to ignore
- Phone call = harder to blow off
- "Making a scene" = you become the squeaky wheel

---

## ✅ The Strategy That Works

### **Step 1: Send Email/Fax (Required for Paper Trail)**
- Send professional RFQ document
- Include deadline
- This creates your documentation

### **Step 2: Call SAME DAY (Within 2 Hours)**
- Don't wait for them to respond
- Call immediately after sending
- Say: "I just faxed/emailed you RFQ [number]. Can you confirm receipt and give me a timeline?"

### **Step 3: Be Direct About Urgency**
Don't be apologetic. Be clear:
- "This is due [date]. I need your quote by [date]."
- "Can you commit to that timeline?"
- "Who specifically will be working on this?"

### **Step 4: Get a Name + Commitment**
- Ask: "What's your name? And when can I expect this?"
- Write it down
- Follow up: "Thanks [NAME], I'll call back [DAY] if I haven't received it."

### **Step 5: Follow Up Aggressively**
- Call again 2 days before your internal deadline
- Don't ask "did you get to it?" 
- Say: "I need the quote by [date]. Where are we?"

## 💡 Pro Tips for Getting Quotes Faster

### **1. Lead with Dollar Amount**
- "This is a $720,000 order" (gets attention)
- Not: "Can you quote 16 trucks?"

### **2. Create Urgency (Real)**
- "Government bid due February 17th, need quote by 12th"
- Not: "When you get a chance..."

### **3. Offer to Make It Easy**
- "I can send specs again right now while you're on phone"
- "I can call back in 2 hours if that helps"

### **4. Use Your Woman-Owned Status**
- "I'm an EDWOSB-certified woman-owned business bidding on government work"
- Some suppliers prioritize diversity businesses

### **5. Name Drop (If You Can)**
- "This is for Oakland County Road Commission"
- Legit government agency = credibility

### **6. The "Alternative Supplier" Pressure**
- "I have another dealer option, but I wanted to give you first shot"
- Creates FOMO (fear of missing out)
