# SERVICE CONTRACT SUBCONTRACTOR FRAMEWORK
**How to Find, Vet, and Manage Subs for Service Contracts**

**Works with:** Transformation Proposal Framework  
**Similar to:** Grainger Call Script (but for service providers)

---

## 🎯 THE PROBLEM

**Service contracts require:**
- Finding qualified subcontractors
- Vetting their capabilities
- Negotiating rates and terms
- Getting firm commitments
- Ensuring quality delivery
- Building transformation story for proposal

**This is different from supply bids where you just negotiate pricing.**

## 🔍 PHASE 1: FIND QUALIFIED SUBS (Before Bidding)

### **Step 1: Define Requirements** (from Transformation Worksheet)

**Must answer:**
- What service is needed? (specific tasks)
- What geographic area? (coverage requirement)
- What volume? (number of locations, frequency)
- What certifications needed? (licenses, insurance)
- What quality standards? (response time, completion rate)
- What timeline? (start date, contract length)

**Example: Madison Heights Lawn Care**
- Service: Weekly mowing, edging, trimming
- Area: Oakland County, Michigan
- Volume: 10 parks, weekly April-October
- Certifications: Business license, $1M liability insurance
- Quality: 48-hour max response, 95%+ completion
- Timeline: April 1 start, 7-month contract

---

### **Step 2: Source Subcontractors**

**Method A: Google/Local Search**
```
Search: "[Service] [City/County]"
Example: "lawn care Oakland County Michigan"
Example: "pressure washing San Antonio Texas"
```

**Look for:**
- ✅ Professional website
- ✅ Google reviews (4+ stars)
- ✅ Years in business (3+ years preferred)
- ✅ Service area matches
- ✅ Commercial experience (not just residential)

**Target: Find 5-10 potential subs per contract**

---

**Method B: Industry Directories**
- Yelp Business
- Angi (formerly Angie's List)
- Thumbtack
- HomeAdvisor
- Industry associations (landscape association, etc.)

---

**Method C: Competitor Mining**
- Search past government contracts on SAM.gov
- See who won similar contracts
- Contact those subs directly

---

**Method D: Facebook/Social Media**
- Search: "[Service] [Location]" on Facebook
- Join local business groups
- Post in contractor/service provider groups

### **Step 3: Initial Vetting (Email/Phone Screen)**

**First Contact Template:**

```
Subject: Government Contract Opportunity - [Service] in [Location]

Hi [Company Name],

I'm Dee Davis with DEE DAVIS INC, a certified EDWOSB prime contractor. 
I'm bidding on a [Service] contract for a [Client Type] in [Location] 
and looking for a qualified subcontractor.

Project scope:
- Service: [Brief description]
- Location: [City/County]
- Duration: [Timeframe]
- Volume: [Number of locations/frequency]

Requirements:
- [Certification 1]
- [Certification 2]
- [Insurance requirement]

Are you interested and available for this scope? If so, I'd like to 
schedule a brief call to discuss your capabilities and pricing.

Best regards,
Dee Davis
DEE DAVIS INC
[Phone]
[Email]
```

**Key Points:**
- ✅ Mention it's a government contract (credibility)
- ✅ Specify you're the prime (they're sub)
- ✅ Give enough detail to qualify interest
- ✅ Don't reveal client name (protect your opportunity!)
- ❌ Don't reveal solicitation number

---

### **Step 4: Vetting Call/Meeting**

**Questions to Ask:**

**1. Capability Verification**
- How long have you been in business?
- Do you have commercial experience? (not just residential)
- Have you worked on government contracts before?
- What's your service area?
- Can you handle [volume] locations?
- What's your current capacity?
- Are you available starting [date]?

**2. Quality Standards**
- What's your typical response time?
- What's your completion rate?
- How do you handle quality issues?
- Do you have a warranty/guarantee?
- How do you track service delivery?

**3. Certifications & Insurance**
- Do you have [required license]?
- Do you have $1M+ liability insurance?
- Can you add DEE DAVIS INC as additional insured?
- Do you have workers comp insurance?
- Any safety certifications?

**4. Past Performance**
- Can you provide 3 commercial references?
- Any government contract experience?
- What's your largest project?
- Any projects similar to this scope?

**5. Pricing Structure**
- What's your pricing model? (per location, per hour, flat rate)
- Volume discounts available?
- How do you handle change orders?
- Payment terms? (Net 30, Net 45?)

---

**Red Flags (Disqualify):**
- ❌ No commercial experience (residential only)
- ❌ No insurance or inadequate coverage
- ❌ Can't provide references
- ❌ Evasive about pricing
- ❌ Unprofessional communication
- ❌ Capacity concerns (too small or overextended)

**Green Flags (Good Candidate):**
- ✅ Government contract experience
- ✅ Professional operation (website, branded trucks, etc.)
- ✅ Proper insurance and licenses
- ✅ Strong references
- ✅ Clear pricing structure
- ✅ Responsive communication

## 💰 PHASE 2: NEGOTIATE PRICING & TERMS

### **Pricing Request Template**

**Send to 3-5 qualified subs:**

```
Subject: Pricing Request - [Service] Government Contract

Hi [Name],

Thanks for your interest in the [Service] opportunity. I'd like to 
request formal pricing based on the scope below.

PROJECT SCOPE:
- Client: [Generic - "Municipal client in Oakland County"]
- Service: [Detailed description]
- Locations: [Number and type]
- Frequency: [Weekly, monthly, etc.]
- Duration: [Contract length]
- Start Date: [When]

REQUIREMENTS:
- $1M liability insurance (DEE DAVIS INC as additional insured)
- [Other specific requirements]

PRICING NEEDED:
- Per location per service: $______
- Or total monthly: $______
- Or [other pricing model]: $______

TERMS:
- Payment: Net 30 after invoice approved
- Warranty: [What you need]
- Response time: [SLA requirement]

Please provide pricing by [Date - give them 48-72 hours].

If you have questions, call me at [Phone].

Best regards,
Dee Davis
```

---

### **Negotiation Leverage Points**

**Similar to Grainger Script, but for services:**

**1. Volume/Duration**
- "This is a 7-month contract with potential for renewal"
- "10 locations weekly = 280 service calls"
- "Multi-year potential if performance is strong"

**2. Steady Income**
- "This is guaranteed recurring revenue"
- "Government contract = reliable payment"
- "No customer acquisition cost for you"

**3. Portfolio Building**
- "Government contract reference for your portfolio"
- "Can lead to more government work"
- "Proof of capability for larger contracts"

**4. Relationship Potential**
- "I bid on multiple contracts"
- "If this goes well, more opportunities ahead"
- "Building long-term subcontractor relationships"

**5. EDWOSB Value**
- "Helps you with diversity subcontracting goals"
- "Good for your business diversity numbers"

---

### **What to Negotiate:**

**Beyond just price:**
- Response time commitments
- Quality guarantees
- Payment terms (can you extend to Net 45?)
- Volume discounts
- Cancellation/no-show policies
- Change order procedures
- Warranty/rework terms
- Communication protocols

---

### **Pricing Analysis:**

**Calculate your margins:**

```
Sub Quote: $5,000/month
Your overhead/management (10-20%): +$750
Your profit margin (10-15%): +$750
Your bid price: $6,500/month

Competitive check:
- Is this competitive for the client?
- What would client pay for direct service?
- What are other primes bidding?
```

**GO/NO-GO Decision:**
- ✅ GO if: 15-25% profit margin + competitive price
- 🟡 MAYBE if: 10-15% margin but long-term potential
- ❌ NO-GO if: <10% margin or not competitive

## 📊 PHASE 3: BUILD TRANSFORMATION STORY

**This is where Transformation Framework connects!**

### **Use Sub's Proof for Your Proposal**

**What to gather from sub:**
1. **Past performance metrics:**
   - Response time: X hours average
   - Completion rate: X%
   - Customer satisfaction: X/5 stars
   - Years in business: X years
   - Similar projects completed: X

2. **Case studies:**
   - "Sub completed X project for Y client"
   - "Delivered Z outcome (time savings, cost reduction, satisfaction)"
   - "Maintained X% quality rating over Y period"

3. **Certifications/credentials:**
   - Industry certifications
   - Safety record
   - Insurance coverage
   - References

---

### **Transform Sub's Capabilities into YOUR Transformation Story**

**❌ Don't say:**
> "We will subcontract to ABC Lawn Care who has 10 years experience."

**✅ Do say:**
> "Our subcontractor network delivered 98.7% on-time service completion for 3 Michigan townships, reducing resident complaints by 67% while maintaining 4.8/5 satisfaction scores. When Madison Heights needed reliable weekly lawn maintenance across 10 parks, typical contractors fail during peak season (July-August) due to overcommitment. Our capacity management system ensures backup coverage, delivering consistent quality even during peak demand - proven by zero service gaps across 840 scheduled maintenance calls for Wayne County Parks."

**See the difference?**
- You're using sub's capabilities
- But framing as YOUR transformation
- With specific outcomes (98.7%, 67% reduction, 4.8/5, zero gaps)
- Showing problem mastery (peak season failures)

## 📝 PHASE 4: FORMALIZE SUBCONTRACTOR AGREEMENT

**Before submitting bid, get written commitment:**

### **Subcontractor Letter of Intent (LOI) Template:**

```
SUBCONTRACTOR LETTER OF INTENT

Date: [Date]

TO: DEE DAVIS INC
FROM: [Subcontractor Name]

RE: [Service] for [Generic Project Description]

[Subcontractor Name] commits to providing [Service] as a subcontractor 
to DEE DAVIS INC for the following scope:

PROJECT SCOPE:
- Service: [Description]
- Location: [General area]
- Duration: [Contract length]
- Volume: [Number of locations/frequency]

PRICING:
- Rate: $[Amount] per [unit]
- Total estimated monthly: $[Amount]

TERMS:
- Payment: Net 30 after approval
- Insurance: $1M liability, DEE DAVIS INC as additional insured
- Response time: [X hours/days]
- Quality standard: [X% completion rate]

CERTIFICATIONS:
- Business License: [Yes/No]
- Liability Insurance: [Yes/No - attach certificate]
- Workers Comp: [Yes/No]
- [Other required]: [Yes/No]

This letter expresses our intent to subcontract with DEE DAVIS INC 
if awarded the contract. This is not a binding contract but demonstrates 
our capability and commitment to the project.

Signed:

_______________________________
[Name, Title]
[Company Name]
Date: __________

_______________________________
Dee Davis, President
DEE DAVIS INC
Date: __________
```

**Why this matters:**
- ✅ Shows RFP evaluators you have qualified subs
- ✅ Proves you can deliver (capacity verification)
- ✅ Provides protection if sub backs out
- ✅ Required for many government RFPs

## 🎯 PHASE 5: WRITE TRANSFORMATION PROPOSAL

**Now you have everything for Transformation Framework:**

### **Section 1: Problem Mastery** (use sub's insight)

**Example: Madison Heights Lawn Care**

> "Municipal parks face a $4,200 per-park annual cost when lawn maintenance 
> contractors fail to show during peak season. The root cause isn't contractor 
> availability - 90% of local lawn services can handle spring maintenance. 
> The issue is capacity management during July-August when growth rates spike 
> 40% and contractors overcommit.
>
> When contractors no-show during peak season, parks deteriorate rapidly - 
> grass exceeds 8 inches within 10 days, triggering resident complaints 
> (average 23 complaints per park per summer) and forcing emergency contracts 
> at 150% premium rates.
>
> Most contractors won't admit they overcommit. But our subcontractor vetting 
> process identified this pattern across 12 Michigan townships - 67% of 
> single-contractor relationships experience 2+ no-shows during peak season."

**What you did:**
- ✅ Identified root cause (capacity during peak, not availability)
- ✅ Quantified cost ($4,200 per park, 150% emergency rates)
- ✅ Revealed hidden complexity (July-August spike, 40% growth rate)
- ✅ Used sub's knowledge + your vetting experience

---

### **Section 2: Transformation** (use sub's capabilities)

**Example continued:**

> "We deliver 98.7% on-time completion across 10 parks for 7 months by 
> deploying a 3-contractor rotation system. When primary contractor ABC 
> Lawn Care faces capacity constraints during peak weeks, our backup network 
> (XYZ Landscaping + 123 Maintenance) ensures zero service gaps.
>
> Madison Heights will achieve:
> • Zero missed service appointments (vs. typical 8-12 per season)
> • Resident complaints reduced by 67% (from 23 to <8 per park)
> • Emergency contract costs eliminated ($4,200 per park avoided)
> • Consistent 4.8/5 satisfaction rating (measured via resident feedback)
>
> This transformation happens within 90 days through our capacity management 
> system: GPS tracking confirms completion within 48 hours, automated alerts 
> flag delays, and backup crews deploy within 24 hours if primary misses."

**What you did:**
- ✅ Stated specific transformation (98.7% completion, zero gaps)
- ✅ Quantified outcomes (67% reduction, $4,200 savings)
- ✅ Explained system (3-contractor rotation, GPS tracking)
- ✅ Differentiated from commodity (backup network vs single contractor)

## 💡 PRO TIPS

### **1. Build Sub Database BEFORE You Need Them**
Don't wait for RFP to find subs. Build relationships now:
- ✅ Create Airtable "SUBCONTRACTORS" table
- ✅ Add 5-10 subs per service category
- ✅ Track: Name, Service, Area, Insurance, References, Pricing
- ✅ Maintain relationships (check in quarterly)

### **2. Get Metrics from Every Sub Relationship**
Track everything:
- Response time
- Completion rate
- Customer satisfaction
- No-show rate
- Quality issues
- **This becomes your transformation proof!**

### **3. Use Sub's Brand Recognition**
If sub has strong reputation, leverage it:
- "Partnering with ABC Lawn Care (4.8/5 stars, 340+ Google reviews)"
- But frame as YOUR system managing them

### **4. Backup Subs Are Critical**
Never rely on single sub:
- Primary can't handle volume → you lose
- Primary no-shows → you're liable
- Always have 2-3 backup subs ready

### **5. Document Everything**
- Letter of Intent (LOI)
- Insurance certificates
- References
- Past performance
- **RFPs often require this documentation**

## 🚨 COMMON MISTAKES

### **❌ Mistake 1: Revealing Client Name to Sub**
**Problem:** Sub can bypass you and bid direct
**Solution:** Use generic descriptions ("municipal client in Oakland County")

### **❌ Mistake 2: Single Sub Dependency**
**Problem:** Sub backs out or can't deliver → you fail
**Solution:** Always have 2-3 qualified backup subs

### **❌ Mistake 3: No Written Commitment**
**Problem:** Sub verbally commits but bails after you win
**Solution:** Get Letter of Intent before submitting bid

### **❌ Mistake 4: Weak Vetting**
**Problem:** Sub looked good but lacks capacity/quality
**Solution:** Check references, verify insurance, test communication

### **❌ Mistake 5: Resume-Style Proposal**
**Problem:** "Our sub has 10 years experience" ← Nobody cares!
**Solution:** Use Transformation Framework → outcomes not credentials
