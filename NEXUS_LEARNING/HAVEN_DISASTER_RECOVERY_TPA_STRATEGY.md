## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

HAVEN is a proposed Disaster Recovery TPA vertical for Dee Davis Inc. The model leverages DDI's existing Contract Management Firm structure to coordinate a pre-built network of disaster response vendors, billing insurance companies (health, property, workers comp) and government programs (FEMA, state emergency management) for coordination/admin fees.

**Core Thesis:** Most disaster response fails because providers scramble AFTER the disaster. HAVEN's "can't say no" model means everything is pre-staged — network pre-credentialed, rates pre-negotiated, insurance relationships pre-authorized, technology platform ready to dispatch. When disaster strikes, DDI pushes a button and the machine runs.

## THE INITIAL TRIANGLE: THREE SERVICE LINES

### 1. HOUSING (Temporary Housing Placement)
**What:** Coordinate temporary housing for displaced families — hotels, short-term rentals, FEMA trailers
**Who Pays:** Homeowners/renters insurance (Additional Living Expenses coverage), FEMA Transitional Sheltering Assistance (TSA)
**DDI Role:** TPA — match families to available housing, handle booking, billing, compliance, duration tracking
**Margin:** 10-20% admin fee or per-placement fee ($50-150/placement)
**Partners Needed:** Hotels (bulk rate agreements), property managers, extended stay facilities, FEMA contractor status

### 2. TRANSPORTATION (Evacuation + Medical Transport)
**What:** Evacuation transport before disasters, NEMT for displaced members to medical appointments, pharmacy delivery
**Who Pays:** Health plan NEMT benefit, Medicaid disaster waivers, FEMA, state emergency management, some health plans cover evacuation
**DDI Role:** TPA — coordinate buses, wheelchair vans, ambulances, delivery vehicles
**Margin:** 15-25% coordination fee
**Partners Needed:** Charter bus companies, NEMT fleets, medical transport, courier/delivery networks

### 3. MEDICAL CONTINUITY (Rx + DME + Home Health)
**What:** Ensure displaced people don't die from lack of medication, medical equipment, or home health services
- **Prescription Assistance:** Emergency fills when pharmacies are down, prior auth overrides, delivery
- **DME Replacement:** Oxygen concentrators, wheelchairs, CPAP destroyed in disaster — replace fast
- **Home Health Continuation:** Displaced patient had an aide — coordinate replacement aide at new location
**Who Pays:** Health plans (Rx, DME, home health benefits), PBMs
**DDI Role:** TPA — coordinate emergency dispensing, source equipment, match patients to local providers
**Margin:** 15-25% admin fee
**Partners Needed:** Mail-order pharmacy network, DME suppliers with emergency inventory, home health agencies in disaster-prone regions

## WHY THESE THREE

```
        HOUSING
       (shelter)
         /\
        /  \
       /    \
TRANSPORT -- MEDICAL
(mobility)   (survival)
```

Every displaced person needs all three:
- **Housing** — where they sleep
- **Transport** — how they move
- **Medical** — how they survive

The three services are interdependent:
- Transport takes patients to dialysis
- Housing determines where home health aide goes
- Medical continuity is the WHY behind the other two

Own the triangle, own the recovery.

## COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE (RESEARCHED MAY 8, 2026)

### FEMA TSA National Contract — CLC Lodging

**Corporate Lodging Consultants (CLC)** holds the national FEMA Transitional Sheltering Assistance contract:
- Administrator since **2006** — deeply entrenched incumbent
- Operates under a **Blanket Purchase Agreement (BPA)** with DHS
- Processed **700,000+ disaster survivors** through **1,750+ disaster operations**
- Manages nationwide hotel network, secure communications, payment processing
- Website: ela.corplodging.com

**Implication for DDI:** Direct competition with CLC for the national TSA contract is not realistic. However:
1. DDI could be a **CLC subcontractor** for services they don't provide (medical continuity, transport)
2. DDI could pursue **state-level contracts** that operate parallel to FEMA
3. DDI could provide **complementary services** not covered by TSA (Rx, DME, home health, NEMT)

### Catastrophe Claims TPAs — Property Insurance

Major players handling property/casualty catastrophe claims:
- **Sedgwick** — Largest, full-service CAT response
- **Telaclaims** — Desk and field adjusting
- **Alacrity Solutions** — Third-party administration
- **BSA Claims** — Florida-focused CAT management
- **Regure** — Technology-enabled CAT claims

**Implication for DDI:** These TPAs handle CLAIMS ADJUSTING. DDI could partner with them as a VENDOR for housing placement, medical transport, and continuity services that they need but don't provide internally.

### Medicaid Disaster Response — Medical Continuity Angle

CMS implements **Section 1135 waivers** during declared disasters that enable:
- Temporary eligibility extensions for displaced members
- Prior authorization waivers for medications and services
- Out-of-state provider reimbursement for evacuees
- Early prescription refills
- Waived provider enrollment requirements

**Implication for DDI:** States need TPAs to COORDINATE these flexibilities — matching displaced members to available providers, handling Rx coordination, tracking eligibility. This is exactly what HAVEN's Medical Continuity component does.

**CMS Resource:** Medicaid and CHIP Disaster Response Toolkit
