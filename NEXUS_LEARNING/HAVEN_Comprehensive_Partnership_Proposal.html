What Happens to Health Plan Members

Medical Continuity Breaks

Dialysis patients can't reach their center. Diabetics lose insulin in flooded homes. Home health aides can't access patients. Prescriptions run out with no pharmacy available.

Transportation Disappears

Members evacuate 100+ miles away. Their car is damaged. NEMT providers are overwhelmed or shut down. No way to get to medical appointments.

Housing Becomes Healthcare

Members in hotels, shelters, or with family. No stable address for home health. DME can't be delivered. Care coordination collapses.

Communication Fails

Members change phones. Addresses invalid. Care managers can't reach high-risk members. No visibility into who needs help.

MCO Regulatory Exposure

Medicaid managed care contracts require continuity of care regardless of disaster status. CMS Section 1135 waivers provide flexibility, but MCOs remain responsible for member outcomes.

Requirement Regulatory Source MCO Obligation
Continuity of care 42 CFR 438.62 Maintain access during transitions including emergencies
Care coordination 42 CFR 438.208 Coordinate services for members with special needs
Access to services 42 CFR 438.206 Ensure adequate provider network including disasters
Emergency services 42 CFR 438.114 Cover emergency services without prior authorization

The Real Risk

When members can't access care during disasters, MCOs face CMS audits, state sanctions, member complaints, NCQA scoring impacts, and potential contract non-renewal. The cost of being unprepared exceeds the cost of preparation.

How HAVEN Works

1

Pre-Disaster: Network Ready

DDI maintains pre-credentialed, pre-contracted network of housing, transport, and medical service providers in disaster-prone regions. Rates negotiated. Capacity committed. Ready to activate.

2

Disaster Declared: Instant Activation

When FEMA or state declares disaster, HAVEN activates. Network partners confirm capacity. MCO provides affected member list. DDI begins outreach within hours.

3

Member Intake: Needs Assessment

DDI contacts displaced members, assesses needs: Where are they? What services did they receive pre-disaster? What do they need now? High-risk members prioritized.

4

Service Coordination: Network Dispatch

DDI matches members to available providers. Hotel placement confirmed. Transport scheduled. Home health agency assigned. Rx coordination initiated. All tracked in real-time.

5

Ongoing Management: Through Recovery

DDI manages member cases until they return home or transition to permanent arrangements. Regular status reports to MCO. Billing coordinated. Compliance documented.

The Result

Members get coordinated care. MCO maintains compliance. No scrambling, no gaps, no regulatory exposure. One partner, one contract, complete disaster continuity.
