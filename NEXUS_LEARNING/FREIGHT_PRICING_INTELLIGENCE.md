# FREIGHT PRICING INTELLIGENCE — DDI FREIGHT 1ST DIRECT

**Created:** May 20, 2026 | **Last Updated:** May 20, 2026 (corrected heavy haul costs, updated all fuel to $5.64/gal actual)
**Purpose:** Cost-to-operate data, market rate benchmarks, federal rate premiums, and DDI markup strategy for every freight lane. This is the pricing brain for Freight 1st Direct.

**Principle:** DDI prices freight using three layers:
1. **Floor** = OO cost-to-operate (below this, OO loses money — DDI loses the OO)
2. **Market** = what shippers actually pay today (commercial benchmark)
3. **Federal premium** = what government pays above market (DDI's real target)

DDI's revenue = the spread between what the shipper pays and what the OO nets.

## SECTION 1: OWNER-OPERATOR COST-TO-OPERATE (THE FLOOR)

**Source:** ATRI (American Transportation Research Institute) 2025 Operational Costs of Trucking, OOIDA cost studies, industry benchmarks.

**FUEL PRICE BASELINE: All cost calculations below use $5.64/gallon diesel (DOE national average, week of May 11, 2026).** Fuel is 35-45% of OO operating costs. When diesel moves $0.50/gallon, OO costs shift $0.08–$0.14/mile. **RECALCULATE when diesel moves more than $0.30 from this baseline.**

These are the REAL costs an OO faces per mile. If DDI's net-to-OO falls below this, the OO can't survive and DDI loses the driver.

**Heavy haul note:** The $2.43–$3.91 is the OO's TRUCK operating cost only. It does NOT include:
- **Pilot/escort cars:** $1.50–$3.00/mile per car, often 1-2 required = add $1.50–$6.00/mile

**HEAVY HAUL PRICING REALITY CHECK:**
- A standard legal heavy haul load (under 80K GVW) bills at $3.50–$5.00/mile
- An oversize/overweight load bills at $5.00–$15.00+/mile
- A superload (150K+ GVW) bills at $10.00–$20.00+/mile, or flat rate $10,000–$50,000+
- These are NOT dry van numbers. Heavy haul is a completely different business.
- One superload move can gross $15,000–$50,000+ in a single trip. That's why heavy haul OOs buy $200K+ tractors — the revenue justifies the equipment.

## SECTION 3: FEDERAL RATE BENCHMARKS (THE PREMIUM)

**Federal government typically pays 15–40% ABOVE commercial market rates** because:
- Compliance overhead (DOD security, FEMA activation rules, reporting)
- Reliability requirements (on-time penalties, service levels)
- Smaller carrier pool (fewer qualified vendors = less price competition)
- Set-aside restrictions (EDWOSB/WOSB reduces competition further)
- Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) requires "fair and reasonable" pricing, not lowest price

### Federal Pricing Structures (How They Buy)

| Agency/Program | Pricing Model | How It Works |
|---|---|---|
| **USPS HCR** | Annual Fixed Price per route | Carrier bids annual price for a defined route (origin, destination, frequency, schedule). Paid monthly. NOT per mile. |
| **DLA freight** | Per shipment / per hundredweight (CWT) | Weight-based pricing, often through GBL or similar. Volume-based discounts. |

### USPS Highway Contract Route (HCR) Pricing Deep Dive

USPS HCR is one of the most lucrative and stable freight programs. Here's how pricing works:

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Bid structure** | Annual fixed price for a defined route |
| **What you bid** | Total annual cost to perform the route (includes all costs: fuel, labor, equipment, insurance, overhead) |
| **Payment** | Monthly (1/12 of annual price) |
| **Fuel adjustment** | USPS provides a fuel adjustment factor tied to DOE national average diesel price |
| **Route definition** | Origin, destination, intermediate stops, frequency (daily/5x week/etc.), schedule (departure/arrival times), equipment type |
| **Contract length** | Typically 4 years (base) + 4 years (option) = 8 years potential |
| **Rate escalation** | Annual adjustment for fuel and sometimes CPI |
| **Key advantage** | Predictable, recurring, long-term revenue — a 4-year HCR route at $200K/year = $800K guaranteed revenue |

**HCR Pricing Formula:**
```
Annual Price = (Daily operating cost × service days per year)
             + Equipment cost allocation
             + Insurance allocation
             + Overhead/profit margin
             + Fuel contingency

Daily operating cost = (Route miles × fuel cost/mile)
                     + Driver pay (if applicable — for OO, this is their take)
                     + Maintenance per mile allocation
                     + Tolls
```

**Example HCR route pricing:**
```
Route: Detroit MI → Columbus OH → Detroit MI (roundtrip)
Distance: 420 miles roundtrip
Frequency: 5 days/week (260 days/year)
Equipment: Dry van

Cost Build-Up:
  Fuel: 420 mi ÷ 6 MPG × $4.00/gal = $280/trip
  OO compensation: 420 mi × $1.50/mi = $630/trip
  Insurance: $25/day
  Maintenance: 420 mi × $0.15/mi = $63/trip
  Tolls: $40/trip
  DDI overhead/margin: $150/trip

  Daily total: $1,188
  Annual total: $1,188 × 260 = $308,880

  DDI bid: $310,000/year (round up for contingency)
  Monthly payment from USPS: $25,833

  OO net: ~$630/day × 260 = $163,800/year ($3,150/week)
  DDI revenue: ~$150/day × 260 = $39,000/year from THIS ONE ROUTE
  + Factoring commission + compliance revenue from the OO
```

## SECTION 3B: FEMA TRANSPORTATION PROGRAM — ACTUAL PUBLISHED RATES & RULES

**SOURCE: FEMA Uniform Rules Tariff (URT) 2024, FEMA Standard Tender of Service (STOS) 2025, FEMA Rate Filing Instructions (RFI) 2025, FEMA PA Equipment Rate Schedule 2025. These are official FEMA documents — not estimates.**

### How FEMA Buys Freight

FEMA uses a **Tender of Service (STOS)** program. Carriers file rates annually with FEMA during the Rate Filing Cycle (RFC). FEMA accepts or rejects rates. Once accepted, FEMA activates carriers via Transportation Service Orders (TSOs) during disaster response.

**Key facts:**
- TSPs (Transportation Service Providers) must have DOT authority, cargo insurance, and SAM/UEI registration
- Rates are filed per PC*MILER mileage — not Google Maps, not actual odometer
- FEMA has the right to accept, reject, or negotiate rates
- Rates are valid for the program year (typically July 1 – June 30)
- FEMA can extend rate validity up to 1 year beyond the filing period

### FEMA Fuel Rate General Adjustment (FRGRA) — ITEM 1300

**FEMA has its OWN fuel adjustment formula — separate from commercial FSC.** Based on DOE national diesel price, checked every Monday:

| Diesel Price Range | % Increase to Linehaul |
|---|---|
| $0.00 – $2.500 | 0% |
| $2.501 – $2.630 | +1% |
| $2.631 – $2.760 | +2% |
| $2.761 – $2.890 | +3% |
| $2.891 – $3.020 | +4% |
| $3.021 – $3.150 | +5% |
| $3.151 – $3.280 | +6% |
| $3.281 – $3.410 | +7% |
| **Above $3.410** | **+1% for every $0.13/gal increase** |

**At current $5.64/gallon (May 2026):**
- $5.64 – $3.41 = $2.23 above threshold
- $2.23 ÷ $0.13 = 17.15 → 17 additional percent
- Base 7% + 17% = **+24% fuel adjustment on linehaul**
- **This is ON TOP of your filed rate.** If you file $4.00/mile linehaul, FEMA pays $4.00 + 24% = **$4.96/mile** effective.

## SECTION 4: FUEL SURCHARGE FORMULAS

**Fuel surcharges are separate from line haul and adjust automatically with diesel prices.**

### Standard Fuel Surcharge Calculation

```
Fuel Surcharge Per Mile = (Current Diesel Price - Base Diesel Price) ÷ MPG

Where:
  Current Diesel Price = DOE Weekly Retail On-Highway Diesel Price
  Base Diesel Price = Agreed threshold (typically $1.20–$1.50/gallon)
  MPG = Equipment-specific fuel economy
```

### DDI Fuel Strategy (How to Be Smart About $5.64 Diesel)

**1. ALWAYS bill FSC separately.** DDI's dispatch margin stays flat regardless of fuel price. The shipper absorbs fuel volatility through FSC, not DDI's line haul.

**2. Update FSC weekly.** Pull DOE price every Monday from eia.gov. Update all active rate sheets. FleetFlow should auto-pull this.

**3. Use REGIONAL diesel prices, not national average.** A Midwest OO pays $5.82. A California OO pays $7.32. Billing national average FSC to a CA OO means they're eating $1.50/gal out of pocket. Match FSC to the OO's operating region.

**4. Fuel optimization is a DDI value-add:**
- Route planning via FleetFlow to minimize deadhead (empty miles = burned fuel for zero revenue)
- Fuel stop optimization (cheapest diesel on the route — some stops are $0.40–$0.80/gal cheaper)
- Fuel card programs (fleet discounts at Pilot/Flying J, Love's, TA — saves $0.05–$0.15/gal)
- Backhaul matching (turn empty return trips into revenue miles)

**5. OO education.** Most OOs don't track their true fuel cost per mile. DDI showing them real numbers builds trust AND justifies DDI's management percentage — "We save you more in fuel optimization than we charge in management fees."

**6. Lane selection based on fuel efficiency:**
- Short-haul lanes (under 250 miles) burn more fuel per revenue mile due to city driving, loading time, stop-and-go
- Long-haul lanes (500+ miles) get better MPG at highway speed
- Backhaul-heavy lanes (returning loaded instead of empty) are worth 15–20% more effective revenue per gallon
- AVOID lanes with bad fuel economics unless the rate compensates

**7. No-bid fuel threshold:** If diesel crosses $7.00/gal nationally and the shipper won't accept adequate FSC → no-bid. OOs can't run at a loss.

**DDI Rule:** Always bill fuel surcharge SEPARATELY from line haul. This protects margin when fuel spikes — the surcharge adjusts but DDI's line haul spread stays constant.

## SECTION 6: DDI MARKUP STRATEGY BY LANE

### The Three-Layer Price Build

```
LAYER 1: OO NET RATE
  What DDI pays the OO (their take-home per mile)
  Floor: Must be above OO's Minimum Viable Rate (Section 1)

LAYER 2: DDI GROSS RATE
  What DDI charges the shipper/agency
  = OO Net Rate + DDI Spread + Fuel Surcharge + Accessorials

LAYER 3: DDI MARGIN
  = DDI Gross Rate - OO Net Rate - Fuel Surcharge passthrough
  + Accessorial margin + Factoring commission + Compliance revenue
```

### Federal Contract Pricing Strategy

**When bidding federal freight contracts, DDI prices differently than commercial:**

### Backhaul Strategy (Profit Maximizer)

**Backhaul management is where the real money is. An OO running loaded both ways costs the same as one running loaded one way.**

```
WITHOUT backhaul management:
  OO runs Detroit → Dallas (loaded) = $3,500
  OO runs Dallas → Detroit (empty) = $0
  OO cost for 2,400 miles = $3,360
  Net profit: $140 (terrible)

WITH DDI backhaul management:
  OO runs Detroit → Dallas (loaded) = $3,500
  OO runs Dallas → Houston (loaded, backhaul) = $800
  OO runs Houston → Detroit (loaded, backhaul) = $3,200
  OO cost for 3,100 miles = $4,340
  Total revenue: $7,500
  Net profit: $3,160 (massive difference)

  DDI revenue on 3 loads: ~$1,500 in spreads
  vs. DDI revenue on 1 load: ~$500
```

**DDI BACKHAUL RULE:** FleetFlow MUST find a backhaul for every outbound load. Empty miles = money left on the road. The auto-dispatch system should never approve a one-way load without first checking for backhaul opportunities.

## SECTION 10: PRICING DECISION MATRIX

### Quick Pricing Decision for Any Load

```
STEP 1: What equipment type?
  → Look up OO Minimum Viable Rate (Section 1)

STEP 2: What lane (origin/destination)?
  → Check regional adjustment (Section 2)
  → Check seasonal adjustment (Section 2)

STEP 3: Is this federal or commercial?
  FEDERAL:
    → Run USASpending benchmark search
    → Use federal rate range (Section 3)
    → Build price from cost-up (Section 6 formula)
    → Validate against benchmark
    → Add federal accessorials
  COMMERCIAL:
    → Check DAT/market rate for the lane
    → Apply DDI spread (Section 6)
    → Calculate OO net (must be above MVR)
    → Add accessorials as applicable

STEP 4: Calculate DDI total revenue per load
  = Dispatch margin + FSC passthrough margin + accessorials + factoring commission

STEP 5: Validate
  → OO net ≥ MVR? If no → raise rate or no-bid
  → DDI margin ≥ 20%? If no → negotiate or find better load
  → Rate competitive with market/benchmarks? If no → adjust
  → Backhaul available? If yes → package as round-trip
```

### Minimum Acceptable Margins

| Scenario | Min DDI Margin | When to Accept |
|---|---|---|
| **Volume/relationship** | 15% | Steady shipper, high volume, guaranteed loads |
| **Standard commercial** | 20% | Normal dispatch operations |
| **Specialized/low competition** | 25%+ | Heavy haul, hazmat, oversize, reefer seasonal |
| **Federal contract** | 25%+ | Compliance overhead justifies premium |
| **FEMA activation** | 30%+ | Emergency surge pricing |
| **Expedited/AOG** | 30–40% | Time-critical premium |

### No-Bid Triggers

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Rate below OO MVR + DDI 15% | No-bid — can't make money |
| OO not available for equipment type | No-bid unless you can find an OO fast |
| Lane has persistent deadhead problem with no backhaul | Price deadhead IN or no-bid |
| Federal contract requires equipment DDI network doesn't have | No-bid or recruit OO first |
| Competition is asset-based carrier on LPTA eval | No-bid — they'll undercut on price |
