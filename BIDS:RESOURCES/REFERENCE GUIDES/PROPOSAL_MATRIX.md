# PROPOSAL MATRIX — The #1 Reason Vendors Lose (And How to Never Be That Vendor)

**Created:** February 8, 2026  
**Purpose:** Read a solicitation correctly. Execute it exactly as instructed. Period.  
**Reality Check:** This is not about being the best writer. It's about following instructions that are RIGHT THERE in the document. Most vendors don't. That's why they lose.

---

## WHY THIS EXISTS

A Contracting Officer said it plainly:

> "Proposals that failed because vendors didn't read ONE instruction correctly."

That's not an exaggeration. Evaluation teams use compliance checklists. If your proposal doesn't address Volume Limitation of Subcontracting, and the solicitation says it must — your proposal gets marked "Unacceptable" before anyone reads your brilliant technical approach. Game over.

This matrix is your insurance policy against that.

---

## STEP 1: DISSECT THE SOLICITATION (Before You Write a Single Word)

When you download an RFP/RFQ/IFB from SAM.gov, it has a structure. Learn it.

### Standard Solicitation Sections (SF 1449 or Uniform Contract Format):

| Section | Title | What It Contains | Why You Care |
|---------|-------|------------------|--------------|
| **A** | Solicitation/Contract Form | Basic info, deadlines, CO contact | Deadline and submission address |
| **B** | Supplies or Services and Prices | CLINs (line items), pricing structure | How to price your bid |
| **C** | Description/Specs/SOW | Statement of Work, performance requirements | WHAT they want done |
| **D** | Packaging and Marking | Shipping/labeling requirements | Don't skip this |
| **E** | Inspection and Acceptance | Quality standards, inspection criteria | How they'll judge your work |
| **F** | Deliveries or Performance | Delivery schedule, locations, period | WHERE and WHEN |
| **G** | Contract Administration Data | Invoicing, reporting requirements | How you get paid |
| **H** | Special Contract Requirements | Unique terms for this contract | READ EVERY WORD |
| **I** | Contract Clauses | FAR/DFARS clauses incorporated | Legal requirements |
| **J** | Attachments/Exhibits | Wage determinations, forms, maps | Required documents to fill out |
| **K** | Representations and Certifications | Compliance certifications you sign | Required check-boxes and signatures |
| **L** | Instructions to Offerors | HOW to prepare your proposal | **THE MOST CRITICAL SECTION** |
| **M** | Evaluation Factors | HOW they will score your proposal | **SECOND MOST CRITICAL** |

| 6 | Section M.1 | Technical rated higher than price | N/A — scoring info | — | Noted | Focus on technical! |
| 7 | Section H.5 | Subcontracting plan required | Yes | Vol V | Not Started | FAR 52.219-9 |
| 8 | Section K | Signed reps and certs | Yes | Vol VI | Not Started | Sign everything |

**How to fill this out:**

1. Open Section L. Read every sentence. Every requirement becomes a row.
2. Open Section M. Read every evaluation factor. Note what's weighted highest.
3. Open Section C (SOW). Every requirement becomes a row to address in your technical volume.
4. Open Section H. Every special requirement becomes a row.
5. Open Section J. Every attachment/form becomes a row.
6. Open Section K. Every certification becomes a row.

**This takes 1-3 hours depending on the solicitation. Do not skip it.**

## STEP 3: DECODE SECTION L (Instructions to Offerors)

Section L tells you EXACTLY how to organize your proposal. Evaluators check your proposal against Section L like a checklist. Here's what to watch for:

### Page Limits

| What They Say | What It Means | What You Do |
|---------------|---------------|-------------|
| "Technical volume not to exceed 15 pages" | 15 pages. Not 16. Not 15.5. | Set your doc to those margins/font FIRST |
| "Single-spaced, 12pt Times New Roman, 1-inch margins" | Exactly that format | Set up your template BEFORE writing |
| "Excluding cover page, TOC, and resumes" | Those pages don't count toward the limit | Don't waste limit pages on admin stuff |
| "Each page shall be numbered" | Number every page | Add page numbers to header/footer |

**PRO TIP:** If they say 15 pages max and you submit 16, your extra page gets thrown out — or your entire proposal gets rejected. There's no "close enough."

### Volume Structure

If Section L says:

> "Offerors shall submit the following volumes:
> Volume I: Administrative
> Volume II: Technical Approach
> Volume III: Past Performance
> Volume IV: Price"

Then your proposal has EXACTLY those four volumes, in EXACTLY that order, labeled EXACTLY that way. Don't rename them. Don't combine them. Don't add a fifth.

### Specific Instructions to Catch

| Instruction | What They're Really Asking | Common Mistake |
|-------------|---------------------------|----------------|
| "Describe your approach to..." | Walk them through your methodology step-by-step | Vendors say "we will provide excellent service" — NO. HOW? |
| "Provide a staffing plan" | Names/roles/hours/qualifications of who will work | Vendors give vague "we have qualified staff" |
| "Demonstrate relevant experience" | Show SPECIFIC past projects similar to this one | Vendors list every contract they've ever had |
| "Address each task in the SOW" | Respond to EVERY task, not just the big ones | Vendors skip the "easy" tasks — evaluators notice |
| "Include a transition plan" | Show how you'll start work without disrupting current operations | Vendors forget this entirely |
| "Provide a quality control plan" | Document how you ensure work meets standards | Vendors say "we maintain high quality" — useless |

## STEP 4: DECODE SECTION M (Evaluation Factors)

Section M tells you how they'll SCORE your proposal. This is your cheat sheet.

### Common Evaluation Structures:

**Structure 1: Best Value Tradeoff**
```
Technical Approach:    40 points (MOST IMPORTANT)
Past Performance:      30 points
Price:                 30 points

"Technical is significantly more important than price."
```
Translation: Spend 60% of your effort on technical. Don't just be cheapest.

**Structure 2: Lowest Price Technically Acceptable (LPTA)**
```
Technical: Pass/Fail (meet ALL requirements or you're out)
Price: Lowest price wins among technically acceptable offers
```
Translation: Meet every single requirement (no gold-plating) and be the cheapest. Period.

**Structure 3: Highest Technically Rated at a Fair and Reasonable Price**
```
Technical: Scored (highest score wins)
Price: Must be fair and reasonable (not necessarily lowest)
```
Translation: Go all-in on technical quality. Price just needs to be defensible.

### Evaluation Factor Weighting — Where to Spend Your Time:

| If Section M Says... | You Should... |
|----------------------|---------------|
| "Technical is more important than price" | Invest 70% of effort in technical approach |
| "Price and technical are equal" | Balance both — don't sacrifice one for the other |
| "Lowest price technically acceptable" | Meet every requirement exactly, price aggressively |
| "Past performance is most important" | Lead with your strongest relevant contracts |
| "Small business participation will be evaluated" | Detail your SB subcontracting plan thoroughly |
| "Oral presentations may be required" | Prepare a presentation version of your technical approach |

### Adjectival Ratings (Know What They Mean):

| Rating | What It Means | What Evaluators Write |
|--------|---------------|----------------------|
| **Outstanding** | Exceeds requirements, very low risk | "Offeror demonstrated exceptional understanding..." |
| **Good** | Meets requirements, low risk | "Offeror adequately addressed all requirements..." |
| **Acceptable** | Meets minimum requirements | "Offeror meets basic requirements but..." |
| **Marginal** | Doesn't fully meet requirements | "Offeror failed to address [specific requirement]..." |
| **Unacceptable** | Fails to meet requirements | "Offeror did not provide [required element]..." |

**Your goal: Outstanding or Good on every factor. One "Unacceptable" on a critical factor = elimination.**

## STEP 5: EXECUTE THE PROPOSAL — SECTION BY SECTION

### Volume I: Administrative / Cover Letter

**Checklist:**
- [ ] Company legal name (exactly as in SAM.gov)
- [ ] CAGE Code: 8UMX3
- [ ] UEI: HJB4KNYJVGZ1
- [ ] DUNS (if still requested): ___
- [ ] Business size and socioeconomic status: EDWOSB/WOSB
- [ ] SAM.gov registration: Active
- [ ] E-Verify: Certified
- [ ] NAICS code(s) for this solicitation
- [ ] Solicitation number
- [ ] Point of contact (name, email, phone)
- [ ] Signature of authorized representative
- [ ] Date
- [ ] Any required certifications/representations from Section K
- [ ] Statement accepting all terms and conditions (or noting exceptions)

---

### Volume II: Technical Approach

**Structure it by mirroring the SOW:**

If Section C has:
```
C.3.1 — Grounds Maintenance
C.3.2 — Snow Removal
C.3.3 — Trash Collection
C.3.4 — Reporting
```

Your technical approach should have:
```
2.1 — Grounds Maintenance (addressing C.3.1)
2.2 — Snow Removal (addressing C.3.2)
2.3 — Trash Collection (addressing C.3.3)
2.4 — Reporting (addressing C.3.4)
```

**For each section, answer these questions:**

1. **What** will you do? (Specific activities, not generalizations)
2. **How** will you do it? (Methodology, tools, processes)
3. **Who** will do it? (Named staff or role descriptions with qualifications)
4. **When** will it happen? (Timeline, frequency, milestones)
5. **How do you ensure quality?** (QC processes, metrics, corrective actions)
6. **What makes your approach better?** (Differentiators — but keep it relevant)

**The Golden Rule:** If the SOW mentions it, your technical approach addresses it. Every. Single. Item. Leave nothing unaddressed.

---

### Volume III: Past Performance

**What evaluators want:**

| Element | Good Example | Bad Example |
|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Relevance | "We maintained 150 acres for Wayne County Parks (similar scope/size)" | "We have 10 years of experience" |
| Recency | Contracts within last 3-5 years | A great project from 2014 |
| Specifics | "$420K annual value, 98.7% on-time completion" | "Successfully completed" |
| Reference | "John Smith, Wayne County, 313-555-1234" | No reference provided |
| Problems | "Encountered equipment failure; deployed backup crew within 4 hours" | Pretend everything was perfect |

**Past Performance Reference Format:**

```
CONTRACT NAME: Wayne County Parks Grounds Maintenance
CONTRACT NUMBER: WC-2024-GM-001
AGENCY: Wayne County Parks Department
PERIOD OF PERFORMANCE: April 2024 — October 2025
CONTRACT VALUE: $420,000 annually
SCOPE: Weekly grounds maintenance for 12 parks (150 acres total)

RELEVANCE TO THIS REQUIREMENT:
- Similar scope: Multi-site grounds maintenance
- Similar scale: 150 acres vs. [this contract's] 129 acres
- Similar complexity: Government client, strict scheduling, quality reporting

PERFORMANCE METRICS:
- On-time completion: 98.7%
- Quality score: 4.8/5.0
- Zero safety incidents
- Contract renewed for Year 2 based on performance

REFERENCE:
Name: John Smith
Title: Parks Director
Phone: 313-555-1234
Email: jsmith@waynecounty.gov
```

**If you don't have past performance yet:** Use your subcontractor's past performance (clearly labeled). This is legitimate per FAR and evaluators expect it from newer small businesses.

---

### Volume IV: Price

**Rules:**
1. Use THEIR pricing format (Section J attachment, usually an Excel sheet)
2. Fill in EVERY CLIN (Contract Line Item Number) — blank CLINs = non-responsive
3. Your pricing must be "fair and reasonable" — not so low it looks suspicious
4. Include ALL costs — don't surprise them with add-ons later
5. If they ask for pricing narrative, explain HOW you arrived at your numbers

**Common CLIN Structure:**

| CLIN | Description | Qty | Unit | Unit Price | Extended Price |
|------|-------------|-----|------|------------|----------------|
| 0001 | Grounds Maintenance — Base Year | 12 | Month | $_____ | $_____ |
| 0002 | Snow Removal — Base Year | 1 | Lot | $_____ | $_____ |
| 1001 | Grounds Maintenance — Option Year 1 | 12 | Month | $_____ | $_____ |
| 1002 | Snow Removal — Option Year 1 | 1 | Lot | $_____ | $_____ |

**Pricing Sanity Check:**
- [ ] Every CLIN has a price (no blanks, no $0.00 unless intentionally)
- [ ] Option years show reasonable escalation (2-3% per year typical)
- [ ] Total matches your cost breakdown
- [ ] Price is competitive but not suspiciously low
- [ ] You can actually deliver at this price and still make margin

## STEP 6: PRE-SUBMISSION COMPLIANCE CHECK

Before you hit submit, walk through this checklist:

### Format Compliance:
- [ ] Page limits respected (count twice)
- [ ] Font size and type correct
- [ ] Margins correct
- [ ] Page numbers on every page
- [ ] Volumes labeled correctly
- [ ] Correct number of copies (if hardcopy)
- [ ] File format correct (PDF, Word, Excel — what they specified)
- [ ] File naming convention matches instructions

### Content Compliance:
- [ ] Every Section L instruction addressed
- [ ] Every SOW requirement addressed in technical volume
- [ ] Every evaluation factor from Section M covered
- [ ] Past performance references complete with contact info
- [ ] All required forms from Section J completed and signed
- [ ] Representations and certifications (Section K) signed
- [ ] Subcontracting plan included (if required)
- [ ] Price volume uses their format exactly
- [ ] No blank CLINs

### Submission Compliance:
- [ ] Submitted to correct email/portal (check Section L)
- [ ] Submitted BEFORE deadline (not on — BEFORE)
- [ ] Correct subject line on email (if email submission)
- [ ] All volumes/attachments included
- [ ] Confirmation received (save it)

### Final Gut Check:
- [ ] Read your proposal as if you're the evaluator — does it answer every question?
- [ ] Have someone else read it fresh — do they understand your approach?
- [ ] Check compliance matrix — is every row marked "Done"?

## STEP 7: COMMON PROPOSAL KILLERS (Don't Be This Vendor)

### Killer #1: Late Submission
- The system cuts off at 5:00 PM ET. Not 5:01. Upload at 4:30 at the latest.
- "My internet was slow" is not an excuse the CO can accept.

### Killer #2: Non-Responsive to a Requirement
- They asked for 3 past performance references. You gave 2. Non-responsive.
- They asked you to address C.3.4 (Reporting). You forgot. Non-responsive.
- One non-responsive on a critical factor = proposal rejected.

### Killer #3: Wrong Format
- They said PDF. You sent Word. Risk of rejection.
- They said 12pt font. You used 10pt to fit more in. Risk of rejection.
- They said "use Attachment J-3 for pricing." You made your own spreadsheet. Non-responsive.

### Killer #4: Exceeding Page Limits
- 15-page limit. You submitted 17. Pages 16-17 get thrown out (best case) or entire proposal rejected (worst case).

### Killer #5: Generic Proposal
- "We are a full-service provider with extensive experience." ← This tells the evaluator NOTHING.
- Mirror their language. If the SOW says "bi-weekly mowing," your proposal says "bi-weekly mowing." Not "regular lawn maintenance."

### Killer #6: No Proof
- "We deliver excellent service." ← Prove it. With numbers.
- "98.7% on-time completion across 840 scheduled services." ← Now I believe you.

### Killer #7: Ignoring the Evaluation Criteria
- Section M says "transition plan will be evaluated." You didn't include one.
- Section M says "price is most important." You wrote 14 pages of technical and priced 20% above market.
