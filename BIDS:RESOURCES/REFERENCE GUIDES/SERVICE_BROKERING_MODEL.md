## THE CONCEPT

You are the **EDWOSB prime contractor**. The agency awards the contract to YOU because:
1. You're a certified EDWOSB (they get diversity credit)
2. You're the single point of contact (easier for them)
3. You manage quality and compliance (their risk goes down)
4. You handle billing and paperwork (less admin for them)

The **subcontractor** does the actual work. They partner with you because:
1. They can't win these contracts on their own (no EDWOSB cert)
2. You bring them steady government business
3. They don't have to deal with government procurement paperwork
4. Consistent, reliable revenue stream

## SUBCONTRACTOR AGREEMENT ESSENTIALS

Every sub relationship needs a written agreement covering:

1. **Scope of Work** — Exactly what services they provide
2. **Pricing** — Their rate to you (not what you charge the government)
3. **Quality Standards** — Must meet government contract requirements
4. **Insurance** — Sub must carry their own insurance
5. **Background Checks** — Sub's employees may need clearance
6. **Compliance** — HIPAA, security clearance, etc. as required
7. **Non-Compete** — Sub cannot go directly to the agency
8. **Payment Terms** — You pay sub within X days of getting paid
9. **Termination** — How either party can end the arrangement
10. **Indemnification** — Sub is responsible for their work quality

## PRICING STRATEGY

**Rule of Thumb:**
- Product resale: 30-100% markup (depends on commodity)
- Simple services (shredding, courier): 20-35% markup
- Professional services (background checks, testing): 35-50% markup
- Bundled services: 25-40% markup (volume makes up for lower %)

**Always price BELOW the incumbent** if you can find their contract price (FOIA the previous award). Price 5-10% below and you still make good margin.

## CRITICAL RULES

1. **Never tell the sub who the end client is** — Protect your business
2. **Always have a written subcontract** — Never handshake deals on government work
3. **Pay your subs on time** — They're your reputation
4. **Quality control everything** — One bad delivery and you lose the contract
5. **Keep records of everything** — Government auditors love paperwork
6. **Invoice promptly** — Government pays NET 30, don't let it slide to NET 60
7. **Build past performance** — Every completed contract = ammunition for the next bid
