# GovCon Email Templates — The Only Scripts You Need

**Created:** February 8, 2026  
**Purpose:** Real email templates that sound like a person, not a brochure

## HOW TO USE THIS FILE

Every email you'll ever need for government contracting is here. Five categories. Copy, customize the brackets, send. Don't overthink it — these are conversation starters, not legal briefs.

---

## 1. MESSAGING SMALL BUSINESS OFFICES (OSBP/OSBU)

Every federal agency has a Small Business Office. Their entire job is to connect small businesses with contracting opportunities. They WANT to hear from you.

---

### Template 1A: Initial Introduction to Agency Small Business Office

---

Looking forward to connecting.

## 2. REACHING CONTRACTING OFFICERS

COs are busy. They get dozens of emails. Be specific, be brief, and show you've done your homework. Reference the actual solicitation or requirement.

---

### Template 2A: Sources Sought / Market Research Response

**Subject:** Sources Sought Response — [Solicitation Number] — Dee Davis Inc. (EDWOSB)

---

- [Requirement 2 from the notice] — Our approach includes [your capability]

## 4. REQUESTING DEBRIEFS

You have a RIGHT to a debrief on federal contracts. Use it. This is how you learn what evaluators actually scored and why you didn't win.

---

### Template 4A: Post-Award Debrief Request (Formal)

**Subject:** Debrief Request — [Solicitation Number] — Dee Davis Inc.

---

[CO Name],

I'm writing to formally request a debrief regarding the award decision for [Solicitation Number] — [Brief Description].

Dee Davis Inc. (CAGE: 8UMX3) submitted a proposal on [submission date] and I'd like to understand:

1. Our evaluated strengths and weaknesses under each evaluation factor
2. Our overall technical and price ratings
3. The rationale for the award decision
4. How our proposal compared to the awardee (at a high level)

Per FAR 15.506, I understand we're entitled to this information for negotiated procurements. I'm available for a debrief at your convenience — phone, video, or in person.

Thank you for the opportunity to compete. Regardless of outcome, we value the feedback and plan to continue pursuing opportunities with [Agency].

Best regards,

Dee Davis  
Dee Davis Inc.  
[EMAIL] | [PHONE]  
CAGE: 8UMX3

---

## QUICK REFERENCE: WHICH TEMPLATE WHEN

| Situation | Template | Key Points |
|-----------|----------|------------|
| Never talked to this agency before | 1A | Introduce yourself, ask about opportunities |
| No response after 2 weeks | 1B | Short, direct follow-up |
| Want to attend their events | 1C | Ask about industry days, mailing lists |
| Responding to sources sought | 2A | Match your capabilities to their requirements |
| Forecasted opportunity | 2B | Ask about timeline and set-aside status |
| Question on open solicitation | 2C | Reference exact section and page |
| Introducing company + cap statement | 3A | Frame why you're relevant to THEM |
| Want to sub for a prime | 3B | Lead with EDWOSB value to their SB plan |
| Lost a bid, want feedback | 4A | Formal debrief request (your right) |
| Lost smaller bid | 4B | Informal feedback request |
| After receiving debrief | 4C | Thank them, show you're incorporating feedback |
| Looking for a sub | 5A | Don't reveal client, state what you need |
| Want to be someone's sub | 5B | Lead with what you bring (EDWOSB) |
| After initial teaming call | 5C | Recap terms, propose next steps |

## RULES FOR ALL GOVERNMENT EMAILS

1. **Use their name** if you have it. "Good afternoon, Ms. Chen" beats "To Whom It May Concern" every time.
2. **Reference something specific** — a solicitation number, a forecasted opportunity, a NAICS code. Shows you did homework.
3. **Keep it under 200 words** when possible. COs scan, they don't read novels.
4. **Include your CAGE code and UEI** — they'll look you up in SAM.gov immediately.
5. **Attach your capability statement** as PDF, not in the email body.
6. **Follow up once** at 2 weeks. If still no response, try a different contact or call.
7. **Never be desperate.** You're a qualified vendor, not a beggar. Professional confidence.
8. **Proofread.** A typo in a CO email is like showing up to a meeting with your shirt untucked.
