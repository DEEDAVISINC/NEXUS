# SUBCONTRACTING PLAN CHALLENGES

**Purpose:** When a large prime contractor on a federal contract is required to subcontract to small businesses (FAR 19.702) but refuses — this is where DDI's formal inquiries and escalations go.

---

## THE LAW (FAR 19.702)

Any prime contractor that is "other than small business" on a federal contract over **$900,000** ($2M for construction) MUST have a subcontracting plan that includes percentage goals for:
- Small Business (SB)
- Women-Owned Small Business (WOSB) — **5% minimum goal**
- Small Disadvantaged Business (SDB) — 5%
- HUBZone — 3%
- Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned SB (SDVOSB) — 5%

**They can't just say no.** If a qualified EDWOSB/WOSB is available and being turned away, that's a potential compliance issue for the prime and the contracting agency.

## THE TEMPLATE (What to Say)

When a large prime refuses to sub to DDI:

1. Document the refusal (dates, names, what they said)
2. Identify the federal contract they hold
3. Send a formal inquiry to the contracting agency's OSDBU
4. Reference FAR 19.702 and the prime's subcontracting plan obligations
5. Present DDI's qualifications (EDWOSB, CAGE, relevant certifications)
6. Ask the OSDBU to review the prime's subcontracting plan compliance

**Tone:** Professional, factual, not adversarial. You're making an inquiry, not filing a complaint. But the effect is the same — it puts the prime on notice.
