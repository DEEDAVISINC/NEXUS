HAVEN PARTNER SERVICE AGREEMENT

This HAVEN Partner Service Agreement ("Agreement") is entered into as of                  (the "Effective Date") by and between:

Dee Davis Inc., a Michigan corporation, with its principal place of business at 755 W. Big Beaver Rd., Suite 2020, Troy, Michigan 48084 ("DDI" or "Administrator"); and

 , a              [corporation/LLC], with its principal place of business at   ("Partner").

DDI and Partner are each a "Party" and collectively the "Parties."

RECITALS

WHEREAS, DDI operates the HAVEN (Housing, Assistance, Vital Emergency Network) disaster response third-party administration program, which provides coordinated housing, transportation, and medical continuity services to managed care organization ("MCO") members displaced by natural disasters, declared emergencies, and catastrophic events;

WHEREAS, Partner possesses the capabilities, capacity, licenses, and qualifications necessary to provide services within the HAVEN program in accordance with applicable federal, state, and local laws and regulations;

WHEREAS, the Parties wish to establish a formal partnership whereby Partner will provide services to HAVEN program members under DDI's coordination, management, and contractual authority with MCO clients;

NOW, THEREFORE, in consideration of the mutual covenants and agreements herein, and for other good and valuable consideration, the receipt and sufficiency of which are hereby acknowledged, the Parties agree as follows:

Article 1 — Definitions

1.1 "Activation" means the formal notification by DDI to Partner that disaster response services are required, triggering Partner's obligations under the Disaster Activation Protocol (Exhibit F).

1.2 "Authorized Representative" means an individual designated by a Party with authority to bind that Party with respect to matters under this Agreement.

1.3 "Business Associate Agreement" or "BAA" means the agreement attached hereto and incorporated by reference, governing the use and disclosure of Protected Health Information.

1.4 "Confidential Information" has the meaning set forth in Article 13.

1.5 "Covered Services" means the services described in Exhibit A that Partner agrees to provide under this Agreement.

1.6 "Disaster Event" means any natural disaster, declared emergency, pandemic, or catastrophic event that triggers HAVEN program activation, including but not limited to hurricanes, floods, wildfires, earthquakes, tornadoes, and public health emergencies.

1.7 "HIPAA" means the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996, as amended, including the HITECH Act, and all implementing regulations.

1.8 "MCO" or "Managed Care Organization" means health maintenance organizations, preferred provider organizations, Medicare Advantage plans, Medicaid managed care plans, and other health plans with which DDI has contracted to provide HAVEN services.

1.9 "Member" means an individual enrolled in an MCO health plan who is eligible to receive services under the HAVEN program.

1.10 "PHI" or "Protected Health Information" has the meaning set forth in 45 C.F.R. § 160.103.

1.11 "Service Area" means the geographic regions specified in Exhibit A where Partner agrees to provide Covered Services.

1.12 "Service Level Agreement" or "SLA" means the performance standards and metrics set forth in Exhibit B.

1.13 "Surge Capacity" means Partner's ability to scale service delivery above normal operating levels during Disaster Events.

Article 2 — Scope of Services

2.1 Covered Services. Partner agrees to provide the Covered Services described in Exhibit A within the designated Service Area. Partner acknowledges that Covered Services may include, depending on Partner Type:

  1. Housing Partners: Temporary lodging coordination, hotel/motel room blocks, short-term rental management, shelter coordination, FEMA housing assistance liaison;
  2. Transportation Partners: Medical transportation, evacuation services, wheelchair-accessible transport, ambulatory rides, stretcher transport, dialysis transport, pharmacy/prescription pickup;
  3. Medical Partners: Pharmacy/prescription delivery, durable medical equipment (DME), home health services, mobile nursing, medical supply delivery, oxygen/respiratory services.

2.2 Service Area. Partner shall provide Covered Services in the geographic areas specified in Exhibit A. Expansion of Service Area requires written amendment to this Agreement.

2.3 Capacity Commitment. Partner commits to maintaining the service capacity (normal and surge) specified in Exhibit A throughout the Term of this Agreement.

2.4 Exclusivity. This Agreement is non-exclusive. DDI may contract with other providers for similar services, and Partner may provide services to other clients, provided such services do not conflict with Partner's obligations hereunder.

Article 3 — DDI Responsibilities

3.1 Program Administration. DDI shall:

  1. Maintain contractual relationships with MCO clients;
  2. Serve as the single point of contact for all MCO communications;
  3. Perform member intake, eligibility verification, and needs assessment;
  4. Coordinate service dispatch, scheduling, and case management;
  5. Handle all billing, invoicing, and reimbursement with MCO clients;
  6. Provide quality monitoring, compliance oversight, and performance reporting;
  7. Maintain a 24/7 disaster activation and coordination hotline;
  8. Provide Partner with necessary member information to perform Covered Services;
  9. Process Partner invoices in accordance with Article 9.

3.2 Activation Notice. DDI shall provide Partner with reasonable advance notice of Activation when practicable, and in all cases shall provide the information necessary for Partner to commence services.

3.3 Support. DDI shall provide Partner with access to DDI's coordination platform, training materials, and operational support as reasonably necessary for Partner to perform Covered Services.

Article 4 — Partner Responsibilities

4.1 Service Delivery. Partner shall:

  1. Provide Covered Services in accordance with this Agreement, all Exhibits, and applicable laws;
  2. Maintain sufficient staffing, equipment, and resources to meet capacity commitments;
  3. Ensure all personnel are properly credentialed, licensed, trained, and supervised;
  4. Respond to service requests within timeframes specified in the SLA (Exhibit B);
  5. Maintain accurate records of all services provided;
  6. Report service completion, issues, and incidents to DDI as required;
  7. Cooperate with DDI's quality monitoring and compliance activities.

4.2 Availability. Partner shall maintain the ability to receive and respond to service requests during the hours specified in Exhibit A. During Disaster Events, Partner shall be available 24/7 for the duration of the Activation.

4.3 Communication. Partner shall:

  1. Designate an Authorized Representative and emergency contact available 24/7;
  2. Respond to DDI communications within timeframes specified in the SLA;
  3. Notify DDI immediately of any circumstance that may affect service delivery;
  4. Participate in coordination calls and meetings as reasonably requested by DDI.

4.4 Professional Standards. Partner shall perform all services in a professional, courteous, and competent manner, consistent with industry standards and best practices for the applicable service type.

4.5 Member Interaction. Partner personnel shall treat all Members with dignity and respect, maintain appropriate professional boundaries, and comply with all Member rights requirements under applicable law and MCO contracts.

Article 5 — Credentialing Requirements

5.1 Initial Credentialing. Prior to providing any Covered Services, Partner shall complete DDI's credentialing process and provide documentation as specified in Exhibit E, including but not limited to:

  1. Business licenses and registrations valid in all Service Areas;
  2. Professional licenses required for the applicable service type;
  3. Proof of insurance meeting requirements in Exhibit C;
  4. Background check results for all personnel who will interact with Members;
  5. Evidence of required training and certifications;
  6. Vehicle registrations and inspections (Transportation Partners);
  7. Facility licenses and certifications (Housing/Medical Partners);
  8. DEA registrations (if handling controlled substances);
  9. References from prior clients.

5.2 Background Checks. Partner shall ensure that all personnel who may have contact with Members or access to PHI have successfully completed:

  1. Criminal background check (state and federal);
  2. Sex offender registry check;
  3. OIG/GSA exclusion list verification;
  4. Drug screening (if required by applicable MCO contracts);
  5. Driving record check (Transportation Partners).

Partner shall not assign any individual to provide Covered Services who has been convicted of a felony, excluded from federal healthcare programs, or otherwise disqualified under applicable law.

5.3 Training. Partner shall ensure all personnel complete:

  1. HIPAA privacy and security training (annually);
  2. Cultural competency and sensitivity training;
  3. Disaster response and emergency procedures training;
  4. Service-specific training as required for the applicable Partner Type;
  5. DDI HAVEN program orientation;
  6. Abuse, neglect, and exploitation recognition and reporting.

5.4 Recredentialing. Partner shall complete DDI's recredentialing process at least annually and upon any material change in Partner's ownership, licensure, insurance, or key personnel.

5.5 Ongoing Compliance. Partner shall promptly notify DDI of any change in licensure, certification, insurance, or any event that would affect Partner's ability to meet credentialing requirements.

Article 6 — Disaster Activation Protocol

6.1 Activation Levels. DDI shall maintain a tiered activation system as specified in Exhibit F:

  1. Level 1 (Advisory): Potential disaster identified; Partner should prepare for possible activation;
  2. Level 2 (Standby): Disaster imminent or occurring; Partner must confirm capacity and readiness within 4 hours;
  3. Level 3 (Active): Full activation; Partner must begin accepting service requests immediately;
  4. Level 4 (Surge): Maximum capacity required; Partner must deploy all available surge capacity.

6.2 Response Requirements. Upon receiving Activation notice:

  1. Partner shall acknowledge receipt within 1 hour;
  2. Partner shall confirm capacity and readiness within 4 hours;
  3. Partner shall begin accepting service requests within the timeframe specified for the applicable Activation Level;
  4. Partner shall provide DDI with current contact information for all on-call personnel.

6.3 Coordination. During Activation, Partner shall:

  1. Participate in daily coordination calls with DDI;
  2. Provide capacity updates every 12 hours or as requested;
  3. Report service completions and exceptions in real-time or within 4 hours;
  4. Immediately escalate any issues affecting service delivery.

6.4 Deactivation. DDI shall provide written notice of deactivation. Partner shall complete all services in progress and submit final reports within 72 hours of deactivation.

7.2 Performance Monitoring. DDI shall monitor Partner's performance against SLA standards and provide Partner with monthly performance reports during active periods.

7.3 Performance Improvement. If Partner fails to meet SLA standards for two consecutive reporting periods:

  1. DDI shall notify Partner in writing of the deficiency;
  2. Partner shall submit a corrective action plan within 10 business days;
  3. Partner shall implement corrective actions and demonstrate improvement within 30 days;
  4. Continued failure may result in termination pursuant to Article 18.

7.4 Service Credits. If Partner fails to meet SLA standards, DDI may apply service credits to amounts owed to Partner as specified in Exhibit B. Service credits are not a penalty but represent DDI's reduced value received from non-compliant services.

Article 8 — Quality Assurance

8.1 Quality Program. Partner shall maintain a quality assurance program that includes:

  1. Documented policies and procedures for all Covered Services;
  2. Staff training and competency verification;
  3. Service delivery monitoring and supervision;
  4. Member feedback collection and response;
  5. Complaint investigation and resolution;
  6. Continuous improvement processes.

8.2 Member Complaints. Partner shall:

  1. Acknowledge Member complaints within 24 hours;
  2. Investigate complaints within 5 business days;
  3. Resolve complaints within 30 days (or provide status update if resolution requires more time);
  4. Report all complaints to DDI within 48 hours;
  5. Cooperate with DDI's complaint investigation process.

8.3 Member Grievances. Partner acknowledges that Members have the right to file grievances with their MCO. Partner shall cooperate fully with any MCO grievance investigation, including providing documentation and participating in interviews.

8.4 Quality Audits. DDI may conduct announced or unannounced quality audits of Partner's operations, including review of documentation, observation of service delivery, and interviews with personnel. Partner shall provide full cooperation with such audits.

Article 9 — Compensation and Payment

9.1 Rates. DDI shall compensate Partner for Covered Services at the rates specified in Exhibit D. Rates are inclusive of all costs unless otherwise specified.

9.2 Invoicing.

  1. During Disaster Events: Partner shall submit invoices weekly;
  2. Non-disaster periods: Partner shall submit invoices monthly;
  3. Invoices must include: date of service, Member name/ID, service description, units, rate, and total;
  4. Invoices must be submitted within 60 days of service delivery;
  5. Late-submitted invoices may be denied.

9.3 Payment.

  1. DDI shall pay undisputed invoices within 30 days of receipt;
  2. DDI shall notify Partner of disputed charges within 15 business days;
  3. Disputed charges shall be resolved within 30 days;
  4. DDI's payment obligation is contingent upon DDI's receipt of payment from the applicable MCO;
  5. DDI may offset amounts owed against service credits or other amounts owed by Partner.

9.4 Rate Adjustments. Either Party may request rate adjustments annually. Rate changes require written amendment and shall not apply retroactively.

9.5 Taxes. Partner is responsible for all taxes arising from compensation received under this Agreement. DDI shall provide IRS Form 1099 as required by law.

  4. Be primary and non-contributory.

10.3 Certificates. Partner shall provide Certificates of Insurance to DDI:

  1. Prior to commencing any Covered Services;
  2. Upon each policy renewal;
  3. Within 10 business days of DDI's request.

10.4 Lapse. Any lapse in required insurance coverage shall constitute a material breach. Partner shall immediately cease providing services if coverage lapses and shall not resume until coverage is restored and verified.

Article 11 — HIPAA and Data Security

11.1 Business Associate Agreement. Partner shall execute DDI's Business Associate Agreement (attached as Exhibit G) prior to receiving any PHI. The BAA is incorporated by reference into this Agreement.

11.2 PHI Obligations. Partner shall:

  1. Use PHI solely for purposes of providing Covered Services;
  2. Not disclose PHI except as permitted by the BAA and applicable law;
  3. Implement administrative, physical, and technical safeguards;
  4. Ensure workforce members are trained on HIPAA requirements;
  5. Report any breach or suspected breach within 24 hours;
  6. Cooperate with breach investigations and notifications;
  7. Return or destroy PHI upon termination as directed by DDI.

11.3 Data Security. Partner shall implement and maintain:

  1. Encryption of PHI at rest and in transit;
  2. Access controls limiting PHI access to authorized personnel;
  3. Audit logs of PHI access;
  4. Secure disposal of PHI;
  5. Incident response procedures;
  6. Business continuity and disaster recovery plans for data.

11.4 Security Assessments. DDI may conduct or request security assessments of Partner's data security practices. Partner shall cooperate with such assessments and remediate identified deficiencies.

Article 12 — Regulatory Compliance

12.1 General Compliance. Partner shall comply with all applicable federal, state, and local laws, rules, and regulations, including but not limited to:

  1. HIPAA and HITECH (healthcare privacy and security);
  2. Anti-Kickback Statute (42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b);
  3. False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. § 3729 et seq.);
  4. Stark Law (physician self-referral prohibition);
  5. Medicare and Medicaid program requirements;
  6. State Medicaid agency requirements;
  7. State licensing and certification requirements;
  8. Americans with Disabilities Act;
  9. Civil Rights Act and non-discrimination requirements;
  10. DOT regulations (Transportation Partners);
  11. OSHA workplace safety requirements.

12.2 Exclusion Screening. Partner represents that neither Partner nor any of its owners, officers, directors, or employees are excluded from participation in federal healthcare programs. Partner shall screen all personnel monthly against OIG and GSA exclusion lists and immediately notify DDI of any exclusion.

12.3 MCO Requirements. Partner shall comply with all applicable requirements of DDI's MCO contracts that are flowed down to Partner, including credentialing, quality, and reporting requirements.

12.4 Government Requests. Partner shall promptly notify DDI of any government investigation, audit, or inquiry relating to services provided under this Agreement, and shall cooperate with DDI's response.

Article 13 — Confidentiality and Non-Circumvention

13.1 Confidential Information. "Confidential Information" means any non-public information disclosed by either Party, including:

  1. MCO client names, contracts, and relationships;
  2. Member information (including PHI);
  3. Pricing, rates, and financial information;
  4. Business strategies and operational procedures;
  5. Technology and systems;
  6. Partner network information;
  7. Any information marked or reasonably understood to be confidential.

13.2 Confidentiality Obligations. Each Party shall:

  1. Hold Confidential Information in strict confidence;
  2. Use Confidential Information solely for purposes of this Agreement;
  3. Not disclose Confidential Information without prior written consent;
  4. Limit internal access to those with a need to know;
  5. Protect Confidential Information with reasonable care.

13.3 Non-Circumvention. Partner shall NOT:

  1. Contact any MCO client of DDI directly for any business purpose;
  2. Solicit business from DDI's MCO clients or their Members;
  3. Use information obtained through HAVEN to compete with DDI;
  4. Attempt to circumvent DDI's relationships with MCOs;
  5. Disclose DDI's MCO relationships to any third party.

13.4 Survival. Confidentiality and non-circumvention obligations survive termination for two (2) years.

13.5 Liquidated Damages. Partner acknowledges that breach of non-circumvention obligations would cause irreparable harm difficult to quantify. In addition to injunctive relief, Partner agrees to pay liquidated damages of $50,000 per violation, which the Parties agree is a reasonable estimate of damages.

Article 15 — Records and Audit Rights

15.1 Record Keeping. Partner shall maintain complete and accurate records relating to services provided under this Agreement, including:

  1. Service delivery documentation;
  2. Member records;
  3. Personnel credentialing files;
  4. Training records;
  5. Financial records and invoices;
  6. Insurance documentation;
  7. Incident reports and investigations;
  8. Quality assurance documentation.

15.2 Retention. Partner shall retain all records for a minimum of seven (7) years following termination, or longer if required by applicable law or MCO contracts.

15.3 Audit Rights. DDI, MCO clients, and applicable government agencies shall have the right to audit Partner's records, facilities, and operations upon reasonable notice. Partner shall provide full cooperation with audits, including access to documentation and personnel.

15.4 Audit Costs. Each Party bears its own costs for routine audits. However, if an audit reveals material non-compliance, Partner shall bear the costs of the audit and any follow-up verification.

Article 16 — Subcontracting

16.1 Prohibition. Partner shall not subcontract any Covered Services without DDI's prior written consent.

16.2 Approved Subcontractors. If subcontracting is approved:

  1. Partner remains fully responsible for subcontractor performance;
  2. Subcontractors must meet all credentialing requirements;
  3. Subcontractors must execute confidentiality and BAA agreements;
  4. Partner shall flow down all applicable requirements of this Agreement.

16.3 Assignment. Partner shall not assign this Agreement without DDI's prior written consent. Any attempted assignment without consent is void.

Article 17 — Indemnification and Liability

17.1 Partner Indemnification. Partner shall indemnify, defend, and hold harmless DDI, its officers, directors, employees, and agents from any claims, damages, losses, costs, and expenses (including reasonable attorneys' fees) arising from:

  1. Partner's negligence, willful misconduct, or breach of this Agreement;
  2. Partner's violation of applicable laws or regulations;
  3. Claims by Partner's employees or subcontractors;
  4. Personal injury or property damage caused by Partner;
  5. PHI breaches caused by Partner.

17.2 DDI Indemnification. DDI shall indemnify Partner from claims arising from DDI's negligence or breach of this Agreement.

17.3 Limitation of Liability. EXCEPT FOR INDEMNIFICATION OBLIGATIONS, BREACHES OF CONFIDENTIALITY, OR WILLFUL MISCONDUCT, NEITHER PARTY'S LIABILITY SHALL EXCEED THE TOTAL AMOUNTS PAID OR PAYABLE UNDER THIS AGREEMENT IN THE TWELVE (12) MONTHS PRECEDING THE CLAIM.

17.4 No Consequential Damages. NEITHER PARTY SHALL BE LIABLE FOR INDIRECT, INCIDENTAL, CONSEQUENTIAL, SPECIAL, OR PUNITIVE DAMAGES, EXCEPT FOR BREACHES OF CONFIDENTIALITY OR NON-CIRCUMVENTION.

Article 18 — Term and Termination

18.1 Term. This Agreement shall be effective for an initial term of one (1) year from the Effective Date and shall automatically renew for successive one-year terms unless terminated as provided herein.

18.2 Termination for Convenience. Either Party may terminate this Agreement without cause upon sixty (60) days prior written notice.

18.3 Termination for Cause. Either Party may terminate immediately upon:

  1. Material breach that remains uncured for fifteen (15) days after written notice;
  2. Bankruptcy, insolvency, or assignment for benefit of creditors;
  3. Loss of required license, certification, or insurance;
  4. Exclusion from federal healthcare programs;
  5. Violation of law that materially affects performance;
  6. Breach of confidentiality or non-circumvention provisions.

18.4 Termination for MCO Contract Loss. DDI may terminate immediately if DDI loses the applicable MCO contract, with no liability to Partner beyond payment for services already rendered.

18.5 Effect of Termination. Upon termination:

  1. Partner shall complete services in progress unless directed otherwise;
  2. Partner shall cooperate with transition assistance (Article 19);
  3. Partner shall submit final invoices within 30 days;
  4. DDI shall pay for services properly rendered;
  5. Partner shall return or destroy Confidential Information as directed;
  6. Provisions intended to survive termination shall remain in effect.
Article 19 — Transition Assistance

19.1 Obligation. Upon termination or expiration, Partner shall provide reasonable transition assistance to DDI and any successor provider for a period of up to ninety (90) days.

19.2 Transition Activities. Transition assistance includes:

  1. Continuing services during the transition period as requested;
  2. Transferring Member records and documentation;
  3. Providing information about services in progress;
  4. Cooperating with successor provider onboarding;
  5. Returning DDI property and materials;
  6. Participating in transition planning meetings.

19.3 Compensation. Partner shall be compensated at Agreement rates for transition services unless termination was for Partner's breach.

Article 20 — Force Majeure

20.1 Definition. "Force Majeure" means circumstances beyond a Party's reasonable control, including natural disasters, acts of war, terrorism, government actions, and pandemics—but expressly EXCLUDING Disaster Events for which HAVEN services are designed.

20.2 Effect. A Party shall not be liable for failure to perform obligations affected by Force Majeure, provided the Party:

  1. Promptly notifies the other Party;
  2. Uses reasonable efforts to mitigate the impact;
  3. Resumes performance as soon as practicable.

20.3 Disaster Events. For clarity, Partner may NOT invoke Force Majeure to excuse performance failures during Disaster Events that trigger HAVEN Activation. Partner's obligation to perform surge capacity during Disaster Events is a fundamental purpose of this Agreement.

20.4 Extended Force Majeure. If Force Majeure continues for more than thirty (30) days, either Party may terminate this Agreement without liability.

Article 21 — Dispute Resolution

21.1 Informal Resolution. The Parties shall attempt to resolve disputes through good-faith negotiation between Authorized Representatives.

21.2 Escalation. If not resolved within fifteen (15) business days, disputes shall be escalated to senior management of each Party for resolution.

21.3 Mediation. If not resolved within thirty (30) days, disputes shall be submitted to non-binding mediation in Oakland County, Michigan.

21.4 Arbitration. If mediation fails, disputes shall be submitted to binding arbitration in Oakland County, Michigan, under AAA Commercial Arbitration Rules. The arbitrator's decision shall be final and binding.

21.5 Injunctive Relief. Notwithstanding the above, either Party may seek injunctive relief in court for breaches of confidentiality, non-circumvention, or intellectual property rights.

21.6 Continued Performance. During any dispute, Partner shall continue performing services unless DDI directs otherwise.

Article 22 — General Provisions

22.1 Independent Contractor. Partner is an independent contractor. Nothing herein creates employment, agency, joint venture, or partnership. Partner is responsible for its own taxes, benefits, and compliance obligations.

22.2 Governing Law. This Agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the State of Michigan, without regard to conflicts of law principles.

22.3 Notices. Notices shall be in writing and delivered by certified mail, overnight courier, or email to the addresses set forth herein or as updated in writing.

22.4 Entire Agreement. This Agreement, including all Exhibits, constitutes the entire agreement and supersedes all prior agreements and understandings.

22.5 Amendments. Amendments must be in writing and signed by both Parties.

22.6 Waiver. Failure to enforce any provision shall not constitute a waiver of future enforcement.

22.7 Severability. If any provision is held invalid, the remaining provisions shall continue in effect.

22.8 Counterparts. This Agreement may be executed in counterparts, each of which shall be deemed an original.

22.9 Electronic Signatures. Electronic signatures shall be deemed valid and binding.

22.10 Survival. The following provisions survive termination: Articles 11, 13, 14.2, 15, 17, 19, 21, and 22, and any other provisions that by their nature should survive.
