### Phase 3: Sun Belt (Texas, Georgia, Florida, Alabama)
**Why:** Large Medicaid populations, MCO-heavy, less entrenched competition than CA/NY, business-friendly.

| State | Medicaid Lives | Notes |
|-------|---------------|-------|
| Texas | ~5 million | Massive, fragmented, high demand |
| Georgia | ~2.5 million | Growing managed care, underserved |
| Florida | ~5 million | Mature MCO market, high NEMT need |
| Alabama | ~1 million | Smaller but less competition |

**Requirement:** State Medicaid enrollment + MCO credentialing in each.

### Phase 4: Later / Research Required
**California & New York** — Largest markets but hardest entry:
- California: 15M Medicaid lives, county-organized (Medi-Cal), CalAIM restructure, heavy regulation, entrenched players
- New York: 7M lives, unions, MAS dominance, complex compliance

**Dee's note:** "Not a big NY fan — need to research underserved states before going that way."

**Criteria for evaluating states:**
- High Medicaid enrollment, few established NEMT TPAs
- Recent Medicaid expansion (newer = less entrenched)
- Rural populations with transportation gaps
- MCO-heavy structure (DDI model fits)
- Business-friendly provider enrollment

## KEY PRINCIPLES

1. **One state at a time.** Michigan first. Prove the model. Then replicate.
2. **MCO relationships open doors.** CareSource, Anthem, Centene (Meridian) operate in multiple states — leverage existing relationships.
3. **Pharmacy delivery stacks on NEMT.** Same MCOs, same credentialing, second revenue stream.
4. **Underserved > Competitive.** Sun Belt before California/New York.
5. **State Medicaid enrollment is required.** Ask MCOs: "What enrollment does DDI need to contract with your plan?"
