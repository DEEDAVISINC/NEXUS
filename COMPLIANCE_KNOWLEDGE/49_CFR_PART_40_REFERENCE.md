# 49 CFR PART 40 — COMPLETE REFERENCE

**Procedures for Transportation Workplace Drug and Alcohol Testing Programs**

**Authority:** U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT), Office of Drug and Alcohol Policy and Compliance (ODAPC)

**Last Updated in NEXUS:** May 19, 2026

---

## OVERVIEW

49 CFR Part 40 establishes the procedures for DOT-regulated drug and alcohol testing. It applies to ALL DOT agencies:

| Agency | Regulation | Covered Employees |
|---|---|---|
| FMCSA | 49 CFR 382 | CDL drivers (trucks, buses) |
| FTA | 49 CFR 655 | Transit employees (safety-sensitive) |
| FAA | 14 CFR 120 | Aviation (flight crews, mechanics, dispatchers) |
| FRA | 49 CFR 219 | Railroad employees |
| PHMSA | 49 CFR 199 | Pipeline operators |
| USCG | 46 CFR 16 | Maritime (merchant mariners) |

**Key Principle:** Part 40 procedures are **identical** across all DOT modes. The only differences are in WHO gets tested (defined by each agency's own regulation).

## SUBPART A — ADMINISTRATIVE PROVISIONS

### §40.1 — Purpose
Part 40 tells employers, employees, and service agents HOW to conduct DOT drug and alcohol tests.

### §40.3 — Definitions

**Key Terms:**

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Adulterated specimen** | Contains substance not expected or concentration inconsistent with human urine |
| **Alcohol confirmation test** | Second test using EBT following a screening test ≥0.02 |
| **Alcohol screening test** | Initial test using ASD or EBT |
| **Cancelled test** | Test that has a problem identified that cannot be corrected — neither positive nor negative |
| **Chain of custody** | Procedures to document specimen handling from collection to final disposition |
| **Collection site** | Place where specimen is collected |
| **Collector** | Person who instructs and assists employee at collection site |
| **Confirmatory drug test** | GC/MS or GC/MS/MS test to confirm initial screen result |
| **Confirmed drug test** | Positive initial screen confirmed by GC/MS at or above cutoff |
| **Consortium/Third Party Administrator (C/TPA)** | Service agent providing drug/alcohol testing services |
| **Dilute specimen** | Creatinine and specific gravity lower than expected |
| **DOT agency** | FMCSA, FTA, FAA, FRA, PHMSA, USCG |
| **Evidentiary Breath Testing device (EBT)** | Device approved by NHTSA for evidential alcohol testing |
| **Initial drug test** | Immunoassay screen used to differentiate negative from presumptive positive |
| **Laboratory** | HHS-certified lab (SAMHSA) that performs drug testing |
| **Medical Review Officer (MRO)** | Licensed physician responsible for receiving and reviewing lab results |
| **Negative result** | Drug test: below cutoff or no drugs detected. Alcohol test: <0.02 BAC |
| **Non-negative** | Lab result that is positive, adulterated, substituted, or invalid |
| **Positive result** | Drug test: at or above cutoff. Alcohol test: ≥0.04 BAC |
| **Primary specimen** | Portion of urine sent to lab for initial and confirmation testing |
| **Refusal to test** | Conduct that constitutes refusal per §40.191 (equivalent to positive) |
| **Screening Test Technician (STT)** | Person who conducts alcohol screening tests |
| **Service agent** | Anyone providing drug/alcohol testing services (lab, MRO, collector, C/TPA, SAP) |
| **Split specimen** | Second portion of urine stored at lab, available for retest |
| **Substance Abuse Professional (SAP)** | Person who evaluates employees who violated DOT drug/alcohol rules |
| **Substituted specimen** | Not consistent with human urine (creatinine and specific gravity) |
| **Verified test** | Drug test reviewed and result confirmed by MRO |

### §40.5 — Who is Subject
- Employers regulated by DOT agencies
- Employees performing safety-sensitive functions
- Service agents (labs, MROs, collectors, SAPs, C/TPAs)

### §40.7 — Service Agent Functions
C/TPAs may perform administrative tasks but CANNOT:
- Act as MRO or SAP (requires licensed professional)
- Make medical determinations
- Override MRO or SAP decisions

## SUBPART B — EMPLOYER RESPONSIBILITIES

### §40.11 — General Employer Obligations

Employers MUST:
1. Use only DOT-compliant service agents
2. Ensure testing follows Part 40 exactly
3. Not accept non-DOT tests as substitutes
4. Report violations to appropriate DOT agency
5. Use only HHS-certified laboratories
6. Use only qualified collectors, MROs, SAPs

### §40.13 — Employer Prohibitions

Employers MUST NOT:
- Stand down employees awaiting MRO verification (unless written DOT permission)
- Use non-evidential alcohol testing devices for confirmation
- Use laboratories not certified by HHS
- Use MROs, SAPs, or collectors who don't meet qualifications

### §40.14 — Service Agent Agreements

C/TPAs and other service agents:
- Must have written agreement with employer
- Cannot act as intermediary to delay or manipulate results
- Must transmit results directly as specified
- Must maintain confidentiality

### §40.15 — Transmitting Results

Drug test results: MRO → Employer (or C/TPA → Employer)
Alcohol test results: BAT/STT → Employer immediately

## SUBPART C — URINE COLLECTION PERSONNEL

### §40.31 — Collector Qualifications

Collectors MUST:
1. Complete initial training per §40.33
2. Complete qualification training (5 error-free mock collections)
3. Demonstrate proficiency
4. Complete refresher training every 5 years
5. Complete error correction training after any mistake

### §40.33 — Training Requirements

**Initial Training:**
- All Part 40 collection procedures
- Collection kit components
- CCF completion
- Common problems and corrections
- Fatal flaws vs correctable flaws
- Privacy and shy bladder procedures
- Chain of custody
- Documentation requirements

**Qualification Training:**
- Demonstrate 5 consecutive error-free urine collections
- May be done with volunteers (mock collections)

**Refresher Training:**
- Required every 5 years from initial training date
- Or immediately after any error requiring correction

### §40.35 — Who May Act as Collector

- Trained and qualified individual
- Employer employees (with training)
- Collection site employees (with training)
- Healthcare professionals (with training)
- Anyone meeting §40.31 requirements

**May NOT be:**
- The immediate supervisor of the employee being tested
- A relative of the employee

## SUBPART D — COLLECTION SITES, FORMS, EQUIPMENT

### §40.41 — Collection Site Requirements

**Location must have:**
- Privacy for urination (toilet stall, single-occupancy restroom)
- Secure area for completing paperwork
- Running water for washing hands (may be outside secured area)
- Toilet with bluing agent (or taped water source)
- Soap in a secure dispenser
- No access to water or substances that could be used for adulteration

**Security:**
- Secure area during collection
- Remove or secure potential adulterants
- Tape off or blue water sources
- Secure soap dispensers

### §40.43 — Federal Drug Testing Custody and Control Form (CCF)

**Current version:** OMB-approved 5-copy CCF

**Copy distribution:**
- Copy 1 → Laboratory (with specimen)
- Copy 2 → MRO
- Copy 3 → Collector (or C/TPA)
- Copy 4 → Employer
- Copy 5 → Donor

**Required Information:**
- Employer name, address, ID number
- MRO name, address, phone, fax
- Donor SSN or employee ID
- Reason for test
- Drug test to be performed
- Collection site information
- Collector name and signature
- Donor name and signature
- Specimen ID number
- Date and time of collection
- Specimen temperature

### §40.45 — Collection Supplies

**Required supplies:**
- Specimen collection container (single-use, 90mL minimum)
- Specimen bottles (30mL primary, 15mL split minimum)
- Tamper-evident seals/labels
- Leak-proof plastic bags
- Absorbent material
- Shipping container
- CCF forms
- Temperature strip (on collection container or separate)

## SUBPART E — URINE SPECIMEN COLLECTION

### §40.61 — Collection Steps

**Step-by-step procedure:**

1. **Verify identity** — Photo ID; employer rep may verify if no photo ID
2. **Explain procedure** — Inform donor what will happen
3. **Donor removes outer garments** — Coat, hat, purse (store securely)
4. **Donor empties pockets** — Display items, leave with belongings
5. **Donor washes hands** — Before providing specimen
6. **Provide collection container** — Sealed, unwrapped in donor's presence
7. **Donor provides specimen** — In private (unless observed collection)
8. **Check temperature** — Within 4 minutes of collection (90°-100°F / 32°-38°C)
9. **Check volume** — At least 45mL
10. **Split specimen** — 30mL in primary bottle, 15mL in split bottle
11. **Seal bottles** — Tamper-evident tape in donor's presence
12. **Donor initials seals** — Date on seals
13. **Complete CCF** — All required fields
14. **Collector and donor sign** — Both signatures required
15. **Give donor Copy 5** — Their copy of CCF
16. **Package for shipping** — Seal in shipping bag with Copy 1
17. **Send to laboratory** — As soon as possible (same day preferred)

### §40.63 — Insufficient Volume (Shy Bladder)

**If donor cannot provide 45mL:**

1. Discard specimen (insufficient volume)
2. Urge donor to drink up to 40 oz of fluid over 3 hours
3. Document start time of 3-hour window
4. Donor may attempt to provide specimen at any time
5. If still insufficient after 3 hours → notify employer
6. Employer refers for medical evaluation
7. Physician determines if legitimate medical explanation
8. If no legitimate explanation → **refusal to test**

**Do NOT:**
- Allow donor to leave collection site during 3-hour period (unless emergency)
- Combine specimens from multiple voids (each must be discarded if insufficient)
- Allow observed collection to substitute for shy bladder procedures

### §40.65 — Temperature Out of Range

**If temperature is outside 90°-100°F (32°-38°C):**

1. Document the actual temperature
2. Ask donor if they have explanation
3. Immediately conduct second collection under **direct observation**
4. Use new CCF for observed collection
5. Send both specimens to laboratory
6. Lab tests both specimens independently

**Note:** Out-of-range temperature is strong indicator of substitution or adulteration.

### §40.67 — Observed Collections

**REGULATORY UPDATE — Final Rule effective June 10, 2026:**
- When oral fluid testing is required but unavailable at the site → directly observed urine collection is mandatory
- Observer assignment terminology updated to **biological sex** per Executive Order 14168
- See: https://www.transportation.gov/odapc/frpubs

**When required:**
- Temperature out of range
- Previous specimen was reported substituted or adulterated
- Return-to-duty test
- Follow-up test
- Collector observes conduct suggesting substitution/adulteration
- MRO or employer directs observed collection
- Oral fluid required but not available at site (effective 6/10/2026)

**Who may observe:**
- Same **biological sex** as donor (updated per E.O. 14168, effective 6/10/2026)
- Trained collector (may be different person)

**Procedure:**
- Observer watches urine leave donor's body into collection container
- Observer must have clear view
- Document observation on CCF remarks

### §40.69 — What Happens When Donor Raises Concerns

**If donor objects to procedure:**
- Explain the requirement
- If donor still refuses → document refusal
- Refusal = positive test result

**If donor claims illness or injury:**
- Provide immediate medical attention if needed
- Do NOT delay collection unless medical emergency
- If emergency delays collection, document thoroughly

## SUBPART F — DRUG TESTING LABORATORIES

### §40.81 — HHS Certification Required

Laboratories MUST be certified by:
- Department of Health and Human Services (HHS)
- Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA)
- National Laboratory Certification Program (NLCP)

**Current certified labs list:** https://www.samhsa.gov/workplace/drug-testing/certified-lab-list

### §40.83 — Testing Procedures

**Initial Test (Immunoassay Screen):**
- Tests for drug class
- Results: Negative or Presumptive Positive

**Confirmation Test (GC/MS or GC/MS/MS):**
- Only on presumptive positives
- Tests for specific drug
- Uses different scientific method
- Must confirm at or above cutoff level

### §40.85 — DOT Drug Panel (5-Panel + Expanded)

**Standard 5-Panel DOT:**

| Drug | Initial Screen | Confirmation |
|---|---|---|
| Marijuana (THC) | 50 ng/mL | 15 ng/mL |
| Cocaine | 150 ng/mL | 100 ng/mL |
| Opioids* | 2000 ng/mL | Various |
| Amphetamines | 500 ng/mL | 250 ng/mL |
| Phencyclidine (PCP) | 25 ng/mL | 25 ng/mL |

**Opioid Confirmation Cutoffs:**
| Substance | Confirmation Cutoff |
|---|---|
| Codeine | 2000 ng/mL |
| Morphine | 2000 ng/mL |
| Hydrocodone | 300 ng/mL |
| Hydromorphone | 300 ng/mL |
| Oxycodone | 100 ng/mL |
| Oxymorphone | 100 ng/mL |
| 6-AM (heroin metabolite) | 10 ng/mL |

**Amphetamine Confirmation Cutoffs:**
| Substance | Confirmation Cutoff |
|---|---|
| Amphetamine | 250 ng/mL |
| Methamphetamine | 250 ng/mL |
| MDMA | 250 ng/mL |
| MDA | 250 ng/mL |

### §40.87 — Specimen Validity Testing

Lab performs validity testing on every specimen:

| Test | Purpose | Criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Creatinine | Dilution check | <20 mg/dL = dilute; <2 mg/dL = substituted |
| Specific gravity | Dilution check | <1.0010 or >1.0200 indicates manipulation |
| pH | Adulteration check | <3.0 or >11.0 = adulterated |
| Nitrites | Adulteration check | ≥500 mcg/mL = adulterated |
| Oxidizing agents | Adulteration check | Presence = adulterated |
| Chromium | Adulteration check | ≥50 mcg/mL = adulterated |

### §40.89 — Lab Results to MRO

Laboratory reports to MRO:
- Negative
- Negative-Dilute
- Positive (with drug and confirmation level)
- Adulterated (with adulterant identified)
- Substituted (with creatinine and specific gravity)
- Invalid (with reason)
- Rejected for testing (with reason)

## SUBPART G — MEDICAL REVIEW OFFICERS (MRO)

### §40.121 — MRO Qualifications

MRO MUST be:
1. Licensed physician (MD or DO)
2. Knowledgeable about DOT drug testing
3. Completed MRO qualification training
4. Passed MRO examination
5. Completed requalification training every 5 years

### §40.123 — MRO Duties

**Primary responsibilities:**
- Receive lab results
- Review CCF for errors
- Conduct donor interviews (for non-negative results)
- Determine if legitimate medical explanation exists
- Verify positive, adulterated, substituted, or invalid results
- Report results to employer

### §40.127 — MRO Interview (Non-Negative Results)

**For positive, adulterated, or substituted results:**

1. MRO attempts to contact donor within 24 hours
2. Donor has 72 hours to respond
3. MRO interviews donor by phone (recorded) or in person
4. MRO asks about:
   - Prescription medications
   - Medical conditions
   - Possible explanations
5. Donor may provide documentation (prescription, medical records)
6. MRO makes determination

**If donor has legitimate medical explanation:**
- MRO verifies prescription (must be valid, from licensed provider)
- MRO determines if medication explains positive result
- If yes → report as NEGATIVE
- If no → report as POSITIVE

### §40.129 — MRO Verification Actions

| Lab Result | MRO Action |
|---|---|
| Negative | Report as Negative |
| Negative-Dilute | Report as Negative-Dilute (employer may retest) |
| Positive | Interview donor → verify or change to negative |
| Positive-Dilute | Interview donor → verify as Positive or change |
| Adulterated | Interview donor → verify (no medical explanation exists) |
| Substituted | Interview donor → verify (no medical explanation exists) |
| Invalid | Direct observed retest |
| Rejected | Order new collection |

### §40.131 — Prescription Medication Verification

**MRO must verify:**
1. Prescription is valid and current
2. Prescribed by licensed practitioner
3. Medication is in donor's name
4. Dosage and timing could explain result
5. No evidence of misuse or abuse

**MRO considers:**
- Is the medication being used as prescribed?
- Is there a safety concern even with valid prescription?
- Does the explanation actually fit the result?

### §40.133 — Safety Concerns

Even with valid prescription, MRO may:
- Report to employer that safety concern exists
- NOT reveal specific medication or condition
- Employer determines fitness for duty with company medical officer

### §40.137 — Marijuana Verification (NO Legitimate Medical Explanation)

**ODAPC Q&A Published May 15, 2026**

**Critical Rule:** There is NO legitimate medical explanation for a marijuana positive on a DOT test.

**MRO MUST verify as POSITIVE regardless of:**

| Claim | Valid Excuse? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| State medical marijuana card | ❌ NO | Federal law governs DOT — marijuana is Schedule I |
| State recreational legalization | ❌ NO | State law does not override federal DOT regulations |
| Foreign country prescription | ❌ NO | Cannot constitute legitimate medical use |
| CBD product ("I thought it was THC-free") | ❌ NO | If positive for THC, result is positive |
| Hemp-derived product | ❌ NO | If positive for THC, result is positive |
| Marinol (dronabinol) prescription | ⚠️ MAYBE | MRO must verify valid Rx and appropriate use — synthetic THC may be valid |

**Key ODAPC Statement:**
> *"Marijuana can never constitute a legitimate medical explanation, even if obtained legally in a foreign country, as it cannot be viewed as having a legitimate medical use."*

**Rescheduling Status (May 2026):**
- Executive Order (Dec 18, 2025) directed rescheduling from Schedule I to Schedule III
- Process is **NOT complete** — marijuana remains Schedule I
- DOT drug testing regulations have **NOT changed**
- MROs must continue following existing 49 CFR Part 40 procedures

**Until rescheduling is complete AND DOT updates its regulations, marijuana positive = verified positive. No exceptions.**

## SUBPART H — SPLIT SPECIMEN TESTS

### §40.171 — Donor Request for Split Test

**Donor may request split specimen test:**
- After receiving MRO verification of positive, adulterated, or substituted
- 72 hours to make request
- Request goes to MRO (not directly to lab)

### §40.173 — Split Test Procedures

1. Donor requests split test
2. MRO directs original lab to send split to different certified lab
3. Second lab tests split specimen
4. Second lab reports result to MRO

### §40.175 — Split Test Results

| Split Result | MRO Action |
|---|---|
| Reconfirms original | Original result stands |
| Fails to reconfirm | Cancel test |
| Unable to test (insufficient, leaked) | Cancel test |

## SUBPART I — PROBLEMS IN DRUG TESTS

### §40.191 — What Constitutes a Refusal to Test

**Refusal = treated as positive result**

Refusal includes:
- Failing to appear for a test within a reasonable time
- Failing to remain at the collection site until testing is complete
- Failing to provide a urine specimen
- Failing to permit an observed collection when required
- Failing to provide sufficient specimen AND no legitimate medical explanation
- Failing or declining to take an additional test when directed
- Failing to undergo medical evaluation when directed (shy bladder)
- Failing to cooperate with any part of the testing process
- Possession of or wearing prosthetic device to interfere with collection
- Admitting to collector or MRO that specimen was adulterated or substituted
- Verified adulterated or substituted result

### §40.193 — Cancelled Tests

**A cancelled test is neither positive nor negative.**

Reasons for cancellation:
- Fatal flaw that cannot be corrected
- Lab rejects specimen
- Split specimen fails to reconfirm
- MRO cannot complete verification
- Collector error makes test invalid

**After cancellation:**
- Employer should retest if testing was required (pre-employment, post-accident)
- A cancelled test does NOT stand in place of a positive

### §40.197 — Correctable vs Fatal Flaws

**Fatal Flaws (Test MUST be cancelled):**
- No collector signature on CCF
- No donor signature (and not corrected as specified)
- Specimen ID number on bottle doesn't match CCF
- Specimen bottle seal is broken
- Insufficient specimen for testing
- No printed collector name (and cannot be identified)

**Correctable Flaws (MRO can fix):**
- Missing information that can be obtained
- Minor paperwork errors
- Transposed numbers
- Missing employer phone/address

## SUBPART J — ALCOHOL TESTING PERSONNEL

### §40.211 — BAT and STT Qualifications

**Breath Alcohol Technician (BAT):**
- Completed initial training
- Demonstrated proficiency with EBT device
- Knows Part 40 alcohol testing procedures

**Screening Test Technician (STT):**
- Completed initial training
- May use ASD or EBT for screening only
- May NOT perform confirmation tests

### §40.213 — Training Requirements

**Training covers:**
- Alcohol testing procedures under Part 40
- Operation of specific testing devices
- Common problems and how to handle
- Documentation requirements
- Confidentiality requirements

## SUBPART K — TESTING SITES, FORMS, DEVICES (ALCOHOL)

### §40.221 — Where Can Alcohol Tests Be Done

- Any location meeting requirements
- Must provide privacy
- Should minimize disruptions
- Mobile testing units permitted

### §40.223 — Alcohol Testing Form (ATF)

- Documents the alcohol test
- Three-copy form
- Completed by BAT/STT

### §40.225 — Devices

**EBT (Evidential Breath Testing Device):**
- Required for confirmation tests
- Must be on NHTSA Conforming Products List
- Prints results
- Approved for DOT testing

**ASD (Alcohol Screening Device):**
- May be used for screening tests only
- Must be on NHTSA Conforming Products List
- Saliva or breath devices

## SUBPART L — ALCOHOL SCREENING TESTS

### §40.241 — Screening Test Procedures

1. Verify employee identity
2. Complete ATF Step 1
3. Open device in employee's presence
4. Perform test per device instructions
5. Read result
6. Show result to employee
7. Record result on ATF
8. Both BAT/STT and employee sign

### §40.243 — Screening Test Results

| Result | Action |
|---|---|
| <0.02 BAC | Negative — test complete |
| ≥0.02 BAC | Proceed to confirmation test |

---

## SUBPART M — ALCOHOL CONFIRMATION TESTS

### §40.251 — When Confirmation Required

If screening result is **≥0.02 BAC**, confirmation test is required.

### §40.253 — Confirmation Procedures

**15-Minute Wait:**
- Must wait at least 15 minutes after screening test
- Do NOT wait more than 30 minutes (ideally)
- Employee must not eat, drink, smoke, or put anything in mouth
- If employee does → note on ATF, may affect result

**Confirmation Test:**
1. New test on EBT (ASD not permitted)
2. Air blank test first (must read 0.00)
3. New mouthpiece
4. Employee blows into device
5. EBT prints result
6. Both BAT and employee sign printout
7. Attach printout to ATF

### §40.255 — Confirmation Test Results

| Result | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| <0.02 BAC | Negative |
| 0.02-0.039 BAC | Positive at lower level (removal from safety-sensitive duties) |
| ≥0.04 BAC | Positive (full violation — SAP required) |

## SUBPART N — PROBLEMS IN ALCOHOL TESTS

### §40.261 — What Constitutes Refusal (Alcohol)

**Refusal = treated as 0.04+ result**

Refusal includes:
- Failing to appear for test
- Failing to remain at site until complete
- Failing to provide sufficient breath
- Failing to undergo medical evaluation (insufficient breath)
- Failing to sign ATF
- Failing to cooperate with any part of testing

### §40.263 — Insufficient Breath

If employee cannot provide sufficient breath:
1. Instruct employee on proper technique
2. Allow multiple attempts
3. If still unable → refer for medical evaluation
4. Physician determines if legitimate medical explanation
5. If no legitimate explanation → **refusal**

### §40.265 — Invalid Tests

**Test invalid if:**
- EBT fails air blank (must read 0.00)
- Device malfunction
- Procedural errors that cannot be corrected

**After invalid test:**
- Conduct new test using different device
- Begin testing process from start

## SUBPART O — SUBSTANCE ABUSE PROFESSIONALS (SAP)

### §40.281 — Who May Be a SAP

**SAP must be one of:**
- Licensed physician (MD or DO)
- Licensed or certified psychologist
- Licensed or certified social worker
- Licensed or certified employee assistance professional
- State-licensed or certified marriage and family therapist
- Licensed or certified drug and alcohol counselor

**AND must have:**
- Knowledge of Part 40 requirements
- Clinical experience in drug/alcohol diagnosis and treatment
- Completed SAP qualification training
- Passed examination
- Completed continuing education (12 hours/3 years)

### §40.283 — SAP Cannot Be

**SAP cannot be:**
- The employee's personal physician
- An employee or agent of the employer (conflict of interest)
- Someone supervised by the employer's drug/alcohol program manager

### §40.285 — SAP Evaluation

**Initial Evaluation:**
1. Employee referred to SAP after positive test or refusal
2. SAP conducts face-to-face clinical assessment
3. SAP determines what assistance employee needs
4. SAP prescribes education and/or treatment
5. SAP provides written report to employer

**Follow-up Evaluation:**
1. After employee completes education/treatment
2. SAP conducts follow-up assessment
3. SAP determines if employee has complied
4. SAP determines if employee can return to safety-sensitive duties
5. SAP provides follow-up report with return-to-duty recommendation

### §40.287 — SAP Reports

**Initial Report Must Include:**
- Employee identification
- Date of initial evaluation
- SAP recommendation (education, treatment, or both)
- SAP qualifications

**Follow-up Report Must Include:**
- Employee identification
- Date of follow-up evaluation
- Clinical determination of compliance
- Recommendation for return-to-duty testing
- Follow-up testing plan (number and duration)
- SAP qualifications

### §40.289 — Return-to-Duty Process

**Before employee returns to safety-sensitive duties:**
1. Employee completes SAP-prescribed education/treatment
2. SAP conducts follow-up evaluation
3. SAP issues return-to-duty recommendation
4. Employee passes return-to-duty test (observed collection)
5. Employer may then return employee to duty
6. Follow-up testing plan begins

### §40.291 — Follow-up Testing

**Required:**
- Minimum 6 tests in first 12 months
- May continue up to 60 months total
- Unannounced, direct observation
- SAP determines number and frequency
- Testing at least once per year if beyond 12 months

## SUBPART P — CONFIDENTIALITY AND RECORDKEEPING

### §40.321 — Confidentiality

**Results are confidential:**
- Only released to employer
- Only released to decision-maker with need to know
- Only released with employee's written consent
- Only released as required by law (lawsuit, DOT proceeding)

### §40.323 — Who Receives Results

**Drug test results:**
- MRO → Employer (or C/TPA → Employer)
- MRO → SAP (if referral)
- MRO → Another employer (with consent)

**Alcohol test results:**
- BAT/STT → Employer immediately

### §40.331-40.333 — Recordkeeping

**Employers must maintain records:**

| Record Type | Retention Period |
|---|---|
| Positive results, refusals, SAP reports | 5 years |
| Records of collection process | 2 years |
| Negative results | 1 year |
| Alcohol test results <0.02 | 1 year |
| Education and training records | Indefinitely while providing services |

## POST-ACCIDENT TESTING REQUIREMENTS

*Note: These timeframes are from agency-specific regulations (Part 382 for FMCSA), not Part 40, but are critical for C/TPA operations.*

### FMCSA Post-Accident Testing (49 CFR 382.303)

**When required:**

| Accident Type | Drug Test | Alcohol Test |
|---|---|---|
| Fatality | ALWAYS | ALWAYS |
| Bodily injury + citation | REQUIRED | REQUIRED |
| Vehicle towed + citation | REQUIRED | REQUIRED |
| No citation issued | NOT required | NOT required |

**Timeframes:**

| Test | Timeframe | If Missed |
|---|---|---|
| Alcohol | Within 8 hours | Document why, do NOT test |
| Drug | Within 32 hours | Document why, do NOT test |

**Best Practice:** Test as soon as possible (within 2 hours for alcohol if feasible).

**Driver responsibilities:**
- Remain available for testing
- Not consume alcohol for 8 hours following accident
- Not refuse testing
